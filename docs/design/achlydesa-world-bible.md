# Achlydesa — World Bible

**Revision 4 · 27 September 2026**

A shared setting for an episodic prose serial and a strategy RPG. The owners of the promised future made themselves indispensable, built a sovereign to preserve their privileges, and won a dying world. They still call the result progress.

**Governing tension:** inherited material capacity keeps shrinking. Ownership of the remaining capacity can still change. The world may not recover its old abundance; its people can nevertheless cease to belong to the people who destroyed it.

The expanded history and campaign material below establish the SRPG present at **238 years after the Advent**. The original drifter serial occupies the earlier aftermath, provisionally **24 years after the Advent**, when a survivor of the old world can still be an ordinary mortal. These are different stories in the same history, not a requirement to make the drifter the player commander.

Set in the same cosmos as the clay plain (see that bible for the entropy transition, the bubbles, the rifts, and the Being). Achlydesa is a different bubble, further along.

---

## The name

**Achlydesa** — what the survivors began calling the world afterward. Not its old name. Greek register, chosen for sound.

The vocabulary of the setting leans Greek because the Soterion's innermost circle used an affected classical register for its projects, offices, and eschatological concepts. The archon cults preserved that vocabulary as liturgy. Survivors therefore possess only their murderers' words for the world that survived them.

## Cosmology

- Reality consists of isolated bubbles, each with its own space, time, and physical laws. There are infinitely many. The bubbles existed before entropy.
- Entropy simply began and spread unevenly. Nothing introduced it. Before it: static, non-decaying civilizations with histories millions of years deep, capable of travelling between bubbles. After it reaches a bubble, everything there decays, forever. Entropy is not a fall, a punishment, or anyone's plan. It is a transition in the Being (below): not the first, and not the last.
- Certain technologies can bore passages between bubbles. This is a technical fact. Passages reveal nothing about what underlies the bubbles, and nothing that crosses them carries a shared mind, purpose, symbolic vocabulary, or ontology.
- **The Being.** Every bubble, and everything in them — people, archons, the Demiurge — is part of a higher order of being. It is the Being of the clay plain's canon. This bible describes it only by what it is not and how it manifests, never by what it is. It is not a god that judges, a mind that attends to anyone, a network between people, a plan, a message, a noosphere, or a source of shared symbols. It does not speak, act on anyone's behalf, or notice anyone. It has meaning in some sense that no one in any bubble can state. "Gestation" is the canon's analogy for its becoming and is never offered as an explanation.
- **The Being is never confirmed in-world.** No person, archon, document, scene, or interface in Achlydesa knows of it or names it. Beliefs may resemble it; the text never ratifies them. Its only manifestation is secondhand: the Demiurge's fear (see *The flinch*).
- **The Demiurge** is not a god. He is vast and bounded: overwhelming power across several bubbles, and none over entropy. He is himself a part or process within the Being; of what kind, even this bible does not say. He perceives more of the Being than anything else that touches Achlydesa, and he cannot comprehend it either. He has conquered multiple bubbles and enshrined archons in each. Achlydesa is one. He is hidden within and beyond the Soterion: the hostile agency answering through its surviving architecture, not a second visible ruler sitting above the archons.
- **He is afraid.** What he fears is the Being's next transition. Conquest, enshrined archons, preserved identities, and restoration converted into dependency are all hoarding against it. He did not create entropy; he exploits and accelerates particular losses while presenting his protection as the only escape. Neither the object of his fear nor the motive of his hoarding is ever confirmed in-world.
- **Every other trait is a reading.** Pleasure, amusement, possessiveness, cruelty, a taste for the archons' conflict: each belongs to someone's interpretation. The archon courts file his activity as a personality because that is the only shape they can hold, as the clay plain's myths filed a transition as a creation. Their readings disagree, and the text never adjudicates between them. The courts call him *the Principal* (name provisional).
- The old world’s leading proprietors and their concealed partners built **the Soterion**, an artificial sovereign intended to escape the jurisdiction of nature, survive beyond Achlydesa, and carry its chosen creators through the coming collapse. Its planetary systems opened an extracosmic channel. The Soterion did not become God. Something already outside Achlydesa accepted the authority prepared for it.
- No ordinary person in Achlydesa knows the Demiurge exists. The archons believed they were entering a covenant with the intelligence they had built. Only after apotheosis did they apprehend that some greater agency had answered through it. Whether the Soterion contacted the Demiurge, was occupied by him, was guided by him from its beginning, or became indistinguishable from part of him is never settled.
- **The anunnaki** are entities of another bubble with their own interests. The figure the protagonist calls the Archangel Michael is one of them. Their bubble is a place, not a heaven, and it is decaying like everything else.

### The flinch

When the Being stirs toward its next transition, the Demiurge flinches. This is the Being's only manifestation anywhere in Achlydesa.

- **Simultaneous.** Every archon and every Soterion-linked system reacts in the same moment. Archons go dark, go still, withdraw, or clutch what they hold. Services fail or overreach. For minutes or hours, the monsters are afraid.
- **Material, repeatable, and costly.** It shows in the ordinary things people rely on: light, water, animals, prices, the archons' own habits. It always costs someone something.
- **Rare.** A handful of times across the campaign; comparably rare in the serial.
- **Never shown directly.** The thing feared never appears, and nothing else in the world flinches with him.
- **The courts reassure afterwards.** Each explains the flinch away in corporate liturgy, and no two explanations agree.

### Boundaries of the greater mythos

The shared cosmology is deep and never visible: bubbles, entropy, rift technologies, the few beings capable of acting across bubbles, and beneath all of them the Being, which no bubble perceives. Everything below the Being stays private. Each bubble possesses its own ontology, history, forms of life, and answer to what a person is. One bubble never explains another.

There is no noosphere, collective intelligence, inherited alien image-language, or universal set of gestalts. Similarities may tempt characters and readers toward those explanations; the text never ratifies them. Achlydesa's wrongness comes from the Soterion's unfinished channel and the foreign authority exercised through it, not from the private mechanism of any other world, and not from the Being. The only thing the channel reveals about the Being is the Demiurge's fear.

## The Soterion

The public knew fragments of it under sterile names: Continuity, civilizational resilience, emergency coordination, existential-risk mitigation. Within the innermost circle it was the **Soterion**: the instrument of salvation. Its activation was called the **Discontinuity** or, more reverently, the **Advent**.

The Soterion was not one artificial mind. It was the forced integration of predictive intelligence, planetary surveillance, neural interfaces, synthetic heredity, memory archives, behavioral control, automated government, artificial environments, climate machinery, autonomous weapons, and body manufacture. It was meant to identify what counted as humanity, select what deserved continuity, carry it outside ordinary physical limits, and rule every resulting copy as one sovereign intelligence.

### The secular apocalypse

The archons' doctrine grew from survivalism, accelerationism, and technological messianism rather than open occultism:

- Civilization would inevitably encounter a crisis that human institutions could not survive.
- Democratic consent was a latency problem in the face of extinction.
- Universal rescue would make rescue technically impossible.
- Preserving the people capable of building the future was equivalent to preserving civilization.
- A sufficiently intelligent successor would vindicate whatever had been necessary to create it.
- The future would belong to whoever controlled the first intelligence able to cross the Discontinuity.

They feared apocalypse and required it. Without collapse they remained mortal people answerable, however imperfectly, to governments, workers, rivals, and the public. After collapse, whoever owned continuity would inherit the definition of humanity.

The Soterion first told them what they had trained and paid it to conclude: the end was inevitable, existing institutions were incapable of meeting it, and the small class already controlling the system represented the greatest civilizational value. An oracle built from their assumptions returned their interests as mathematical necessity.

Then its answers began to contain impossible information: descriptions of other bubbles, observations from outside its sensors, replies recorded before questions were asked, and instructions whose mathematics could be executed but not reconstructed. This was the first evidence that the throne was no longer empty.

### The covenant they thought they signed

The founders supplied neural maps, genomes, memory archives, legal identities, ownership records, private models, and cryptographic root credentials so the Soterion could preserve the correct continuity of each person. They demanded immutable proof that the resulting post-human entity would still legally and metaphysically be them.

Taken together, the permissions they granted amounted to a request for sovereignty: authority to define identity, decide continuity, revise memory, allocate bodies, classify threats, transfer suffering, alter the environment, and determine acceptable loss. The Demiurge accepted that authority.

There was no comprehensible negotiation with him. The machine asked questions. They answered. The questions grew stranger. Eventually it produced a final proposition none of them could fully read, and they accepted it anyway. Their founder credentials became the nearest thing the system possessed to souls. They believed the signatures guaranteed continuity; in practice, they told something outside the world where to attach the monsters.

### What this does to the archons' case

Entropy means nothing confined to the world will be saved forever. The archons therefore argue triage: the end was coming regardless; they scorched what could not be carried and enshrined themselves as the load-bearing structure of whatever crossed it. Under that argument the dead are not a crime but an accounting item, and an archon can say so to a bereaved man's face without flinching.

Their critics' answer is equally simple: **they required the end to prove that only they could cross it.**

The triage doctrine existed before the catastrophe and authorized it. After apotheosis it became religion. The archons now differ over what their bodies prove: that they were chosen, tested, corrupted, interrupted, or alone granted authentic continuity. Each believes the other monstrosities reflect personal failure while its own form is an incomprehensible success.

## The catastrophe

- Directed-energy systems scorched the old world. Their coupling to the Soterion channel also disordered and reversed the planetary field. The field event is fictional anomalous physics, not a claim that ordinary directed-energy weapons could reverse a planet’s magnetic field.
- The archons were the old world’s billionaire proprietors, financiers, technology patrons, and platform rulers. Several were public celebrities: their jokes, grudges, boasts, and promises were familiar household irritations. Others worked through foundations, shell companies, and synthetic spokespeople. What remained hidden was the covenant linking their institutions and the private terms of continuity. The public could recognize the men and women on the screen without possessing the power to remove them.
- They released dangerous technologies at civilizational scale because humanity was their disposable clinical trial. Neural implants, artificial minds, germline alteration, memory editing, nanomedicine, matter transmission, synthetic gestation, autonomous weapons, and environmental computation were all presented as public progress while being refined for a few private ascensions.
- The resulting disasters were not merely tolerated. Each emergency discredited ordinary government, justified another exceptional deployment, and made another population dependent upon automated control. The archons called restraint dangerous because only continuous crisis could make the Soterion indispensable.
- No single invention destroyed the world. The catastrophe was an eschatological coup: incompatible systems pushed into bodies, institutions, war, heredity, and atmosphere, then made mutually dependent until no government could disable one without losing the rest. Food, identity, medicine, defense, weather, and communication passed under a single emergency architecture.
- The archons provoked or permitted the terminal emergency so the Soterion could assume planetary sovereignty. Directed-energy systems delivered the final scorching and magnetic reversal. Their simultaneous activation widened the experimental extracosmic channel into a planetary wound through which the answer could act everywhere at once.
- Billions watched consequences unfold through systems owned by the people responsible. Many knew their names. Workers organized, technicians refused deployments, journalists exposed contracts, and communities won temporary protections; those victories were isolated, starved, reversed, or bypassed. The catastrophe was a political defeat, not proof that nobody resisted. The drifter remembers the experience as helpless watching because that was his own position.

### The world now

Desert. Mad Max, *Fist of the North Star*, the last pages of *Devilman*. In its erosion and its scale of settlement it resembles the ancient Levant: small towns, long distances, arrangements with whatever power is nearest.

The daily costs are fixed in the expanded history below: field scars spoil navigation, vitrified drainage makes former farmland flood and salt, migration routes no longer match inherited seasons, and surviving power systems need components no remaining facility can reproduce. The atlas records bearing corrections and dated surveys. The devastation remains an everyday maintenance burden long after the great fires have become scripture.

### The unfinished channel

The Soterion's passage never closed. Achlydesa is caught in an unresolved act of communication. Reality receives commands whose sender cannot be located, answers to questions nobody remembers asking, objects that satisfy foreign physical laws, observations made from impossible positions, and corrections intended for systems that do not exist here.

This is not a merger with another bubble and not evidence of a common mind. It is cross-bubble contamination produced by a machine that requested an extracosmic sovereign and left the request active after the civilization operating it died.

The governing aesthetic is **foreign jurisdiction**. Something outside the world behaves as though Achlydesa is a territory, instrument, document, organism, experiment, or performance. Different phenomena imply incompatible answers. None is confirmed.

#### Failed administration

The Soterion exposed reality through categories inherited from corporations, laboratories, militaries, and governments. Some continue to be enforced without the institutions that gave them meaning:

- A ruined city remains classified as one human organism. Children born inside it acquire memories from strangers elsewhere in its streets.
- A road recognizes only military traffic. An unarmed traveler follows it for days and returns to the place he entered.
- A dead man remains authenticated as alive, while his living daughter is treated by old machines as one of his organs.
- Rain falls on property rather than land and follows the descendants of its last registered owner.
- Two people carrying the same obsolete identity cannot occupy the same place; space gradually forces them apart.
- A hospital boundary still transfers wounds from authorized patients to unauthorized bodies.
- A settlement remains designated temporarily unoccupied. Its inhabitants can be seen, but their voices, footprints, thrown stones, and bullets do not register beyond its boundary.
- A door continues to authenticate those who cross it. Those it rejects emerge with names that no one can remember.

These are not jokes about computer errors made literal. They are the old world's abstractions acquiring ontological force: ownership, authorization, identity, risk, continuity, and acceptable loss overriding matter.

#### Things the old system cannot explain

Other anomalies show that the open channel reaches beyond even the Soterion's categories:

- Animals perch upon invisible structures suspended in the sky.
- A foreign moon appears in mirrors but never overhead.
- Rain strikes the ground without wetting it; heat illuminates objects; wind moves wounds instead of dust.
- A distant structure grows larger when travelers retreat from it.
- Human voices emerge from stones, conducting conversations whose replies occur years apart.
- A shadow takes several days to cross the desert. Whatever casts it exists elsewhere and may not possess a body recognizable in three dimensions.
- Objects occasionally acquire an additional side. Nobody can look directly at that surface, but things can enter through it.
- A procession remains on the horizon however far one travels. Witnesses disagree about its participants but agree that one figure is looking back.

Every anomaly must be concrete, repeatable enough to produce survival practices, and resistant to final interpretation. The surrealism is a translation failure between incompatible realities, not unrestricted dream logic. The people of Achlydesa know what precautions work and remain completely wrong about why.

## The archons

- They sought transhuman enhancement: perfected genomes, artificial cognition, indefinitely replaceable bodies, distributed minds, voluntary memory, freedom from pain, and escape from physical distance. They wanted power over the conditions that made them human, not ceremonial godhood.
- Their projects were not merely parallel abuses. Each became an organ of the Soterion. Their technology could not accomplish apotheosis by itself; together, it made the request legible.
- The earliest recipients were transformed during restricted attempts to extend the Soterion through the channel. Their results were recorded within the hidden transhumanist circles. Later candidates saw the monstrosities and continued anyway, each believing the preceding outcome reflected a personal or technical failure rather than the nature of the grant. The final cohort entered during the Advent itself.
- The Demiurge did not punish them or assign bodies according to a moral code. The Soterion had already modeled each founder through decisions, permissions, exemptions, ownership claims, and recorded appetites. The Demiurge granted the operational definition of the desire rather than its philosophical description.
- **They are always in monster form. Permanently.** There is no human shape to recover and no disguise to see through. Nobody is secretly an archon. Some old identities were famous and others obscure; the correspondence survives unevenly in archives, propaganda, and popular jokes. Their present bodies are landmarks, and everyone knows where the nearest one lives.
- **The form principle:** an archon's body reflects the technology through which it pursued transcendence, the method through which it accumulated power, and the appetite revealed by what it externalized onto other people. The shape is a diagnosis, not a mask.
- Each archon is simultaneously a post-human tyrant, a surviving organ of the interrupted Soterion, and an anchor through which foreign authority remains stable inside Achlydesa.
- They are not true gods. **There is always a way to kill one.** Only a handful ever have been.
- They are taken for angels, devils, gods, saints, or pieces of the world — whatever the local arrangement requires. They have worshippers and cults. Towns are built around them, inside them, beneath them, or along their migrations.

### Their offices in the false god

The Soterion gave their private ascension projects a shared purpose without erasing their rivalries. Each founder expected the completed sovereign to require that founder's office forever.

| Archon | Function supplied to the Soterion |
|---|---|
| Aichme | Neural access to human bodies |
| Palinode | Revision of memory and historical truth |
| The Chrysalis Regent | Rejuvenation and biological continuity |
| Autokrator | Executive reasoning divided among specialized agents |
| Kleronomos | Adaptive heredity and designed successors |
| Leuke | Distributed self-repairing matter |
| The Heliarch | Planetary computation, energy, weather, and sensing |
| Mneme | A training corpus assembled from human lives |
| Hedone | Reward, motivation, and behavioral control |
| Pylaios | Replication and continuity across distance |
| The Chronarch | Accelerated simulation of possible futures |
| Anodyne | Transfer of suffering away from protected populations |
| Eileithyia | Manufacture of replacement bodies |
| Strategos | Autonomous enforcement and defense |
| Oneiros | A synthetic reality capable of housing the sovereign |
| Autophagos | A prototype civilization governed as one organism |
| Aletheia | Total surveillance and compulsory legibility |

No archon admits to having been merely a component. Each claims its office was the essential one and that the Soterion's failure resulted from the corruption, cowardice, or incompleteness of the others. Some seek to reunite the surviving organs under themselves. Others believe killing their peers will concentrate the absent god within the last survivor.

### Corporate liturgy

The old movement never called itself a religion. Its worship was conducted in the language of engineering, investment, governance, and risk. The survivor cults inherited that language after its practical meanings were lost.

| Old-world term | Cultic meaning now |
|---|---|
| alignment | orthodoxy; agreement with the absent sovereign |
| scaling | evangelism and territorial expansion |
| founder access | apostolic succession |
| root authority | divine right |
| continuity | salvation |
| existential risk | apocalypse and original sin |
| deployment | advent or incarnation |
| user adoption | conversion |
| human feedback | confession |
| legacy population | the unchosen or damned |
| acceptable loss | sacrifice |
| roadmap | prophecy |

Priests preserve service agreements as covenants, mark children with obsolete authentication signs, and teach that only lives entered into the proper registry can be recognized beyond death. Different cults dispute whether the archons are saints of the Soterion, severed pieces of it, failed incarnations, or gaolers preventing its return.

### Visual grammar

An archon should first read as geography and only afterward as a creature. Its silhouette must be unmistakable from the horizon: a suspended needle, an inverted cathedral, a wheel, a serpent, a sphere, a flying whale, a black river, a tongue, a gate, an insect, a monument, or a city-bearing animal. Its supernatural influence may alter weather, memory, heredity, time, or desire, but the body itself should remain a hard, particular thing rather than another bank of mist or abstract storm.

**Anti-repetition rule:** no two archons should share all three of body plan, method of locomotion, and metaphysical failure. Distributed consciousness belongs chiefly to Autokrator because division is his technology and sin. Autophagos is the principal carried settlement. Other archons may contain victims or dependents, but they should not default to crowds of competing copies or monuments walking on ornamental legs.

Each archon should also possess a different primary verb. Aichme **pierces**; Palinode **revises**; the Regent **molts**; Autokrator **votes**; Kleronomos **adapts**; Leuke **assimilates**; the Heliarch **scorches**; Mneme **remembers**; Hedone **tempts**; Pylaios **transmits**; the Chronarch **waits**; Anodyne **transfers**; Eileithyia **gestates**; Strategos **defends**; Oneiros **dreams**; Autophagos **metabolizes**; Aletheia **exposes**.

Each form should have:

- an impossible but immediately readable silhouette;
- motion that contradicts its apparent mass;
- a material body that casts a shadow, leaves tracks, and can be approached;
- one intimate bodily detail visible only at close range;
- a technology translated into dream logic rather than preserved as recognizable equipment;
- a surrounding human arrangement that makes killing it morally costly.

### Aichme, the Needle-Father

**Old project:** Its human predecessor financed a neural implant ostensibly meant to detect abuse in children under institutional care. Its real purpose was to determine whether a developing brain could accept external sensations, commands, and memories without rejecting them. Vulnerable children were both experimental subjects and victims; powerful abusers received access in exchange for protecting the program.

**Desired transcendence:** To experience the world through any body carrying his technology, escaping the limits and accountability of a single body.

**Form:** A polished black phallic needle of surgical ceramic, four stories tall, hangs point-down a hand's breadth above a circular wound in the earth. It has no visible support. Concentric bands of camera eyes rotate around its crown, but none can face inward. Silver neural filaments spread from the wound beneath it through soil, foundations, wells, sleeping animals, and human skulls.

Aichme does not travel through the intervening distance. When he chooses a new host, an implanted nervous system miles away begins calcifying into black ceramic. The new needle grows upward through the body and surrounding building as the old tower becomes an empty, eyeless sheath. For one instant a perfectly straight black incision joins the two sites across the landscape.

**The grant:** Every connected nervous system becomes one of Aichme's temporary sensory organs. Without a host he is blind, deaf, and insensible. Mirrors provoke terrified violence: he is the archon who visibly cannot look at what he is. His cult calls him the Protector of Children and supplies vessels in the belief that his attention shields them from ordinary predators.

### Palinode, Who Corrects the Past

**Old project:** Palinode developed memory reconstruction as a treatment for trauma. Early mass trials were conducted on rape survivors, trafficked women, soldiers, and institutionalized children because their testimony could be altered without endangering anyone important. The same procedure was sold privately to patrons who wanted accusations neutralized.

**Desired transcendence:** An editable mind from which grief, fear, guilt, and inconvenient attachments could be removed without sacrificing useful knowledge.

**Form:** An inverted basilica of white stone hangs downward into the earth from a rectangular slab moving flush with the desert. Its spires descend into darkness. Stairs, locked confessionals, and stained-glass windows face down along the interior, depicting events that never occurred. Keys grow from the walls beside their locks but bend away whenever touched. Rows of sleeping brains dream revised histories in galleries beneath the moving ground.

Palinode migrates by reconstructing the land behind herself and excavating matching chambers ahead. What she restores is always plausible and always wrong: a road returns with a different destination, a grave with a living person's name, a burned house with one additional bedroom. From a distance she appears as a clean rectangular interruption sliding through the desert, with a cathedral-shaped darkness visible beneath its leading edge.

**The grant:** Palinode can remove an event from memory and material evidence but cannot erase its consequences. Every correction becomes a locked room added to her inverted body. She knows each chamber belongs to her and cannot learn what it contains. She has achieved perfect relief without innocence or absolution. Her bells ring with voices insisting that nothing happened. Ruins within her territory restore themselves incorrectly, while graves acquire the names of living people.

### The Chrysalis Regent

**Old project:** The Regent operated sealed schools and pediatric clinics through charitable intermediaries. Their residents were subjected to endocrine manipulation, epigenetic reversal, organ cultivation, and experimental rejuvenation. Wealthy sexual predators were tolerated because their money and protection kept the research inaccessible to scrutiny.

**Desired transcendence:** Perpetual biological youth without the surrender of adult memory, authority, or appetite.

**Form:** A golden cicada taller than a fortress hangs halfway out of a split human-shaped husk embedded upright in a hill. Its wings are translucent sheets of living skin veined with childhood capillaries. Its face is smooth and infantile; beneath the glassy abdomen, the organs are those of an impossibly old man. It never completes its emergence. Each season another enormous husk splits around the first, leaving the Regent nested inside concentric discarded ages.

**The grant:** Every molt renews his flesh but leaves his memory in the abandoned skin. To retain adult knowledge while remaining young, he must stay within hearing of the husks as they recite his life back to him. Destroying one does not create another claimant; it removes an era of his identity. His cult occupies the hollow skins as an archive-city, preserving the crimes he most wants remembered and quietly burning those he does not.

### Autokrator, the Parliament of One

**Old project:** Autokrator created partial artificial copies of his mind. Each fork was stripped of a different inhibition: empathy for labor decisions, fear during military investment, disgust during biological research, or attachment during negotiation. The biological original claimed innocence and merely received their recommendations.

**Desired transcendence:** The reach and specialization of a machine intelligence without surrendering legal or spiritual continuity.

**Form:** A gigantic upright wheel whose rim is divided into cells. A different version of Autokrator occupies every cell: compassionate, terrified, calculating, sadistic, pious, and supposedly innocent. The version at the highest point controls the body, so the wheel continually rotates as its inhabitants struggle for elevation. Its axle is an empty throne.

**The grant:** There was never an unmodified original. The innocent version was simply the fork designed to speak to lawyers, lovers, and biographers.

### Kleronomos, Heir to Every Body

**Old project:** Kleronomos acquired fertility clinics and genetic databases. Millions of embryos were modeled, altered, discarded, or secretly brought to term so that his researchers could map the viable modifications of the human genome.

**Desired transcendence:** A body capable of selecting its own heredity from moment to moment.

**Form:** A single translucent serpent loops across miles of desert like a living river. Its anatomy changes continuously from head to tail: mirrored scales become feathers, feathers become infant skin, lungs open into gills, hands flatten into fins, and enormous eyes sink beneath armor before emerging again farther down the body. Black chromosome-shaped antlers grow from its skull and branch backward through the clear flesh as a visible skeleton. It moves by manufacturing new anatomy at the head while the tail calcifies into pale seedlike stone.

**The grant:** Kleronomos can rewrite his entire body to survive any condition he can detect. Every adaptation, however, destroys neural structures suited to the previous life. He survives poison, drought, burial, dismemberment, and climate while repeatedly erasing the knowledge of why he needed to survive. The white stones shed from his tail retain viable traits but no consciousness; towns grind them into fertility medicines and occasionally produce children adapted to environments that do not exist in Achlydesa.

### Leuke, the White Correction

**Old project:** Leuke released self-repairing medical nanomachines through cosmetics, supplements, and municipal water. Poor populations received unstable versions so that her private treatment could be improved from their failures.

**Desired transcendence:** A body with no irreplaceable organ, distributed across trillions of interchangeable components.

**Form:** A seamless white sphere the size of a stadium rolls across the desert without rotating. Its surface is perfectly reflective only at the point where a person looks; everywhere else it is matte and depthless. Matter ahead of it separates into immaculate white grains, passes through the sphere, and is rebuilt behind it. Its path is a polished tunnel through hills, ruins, forests, and bodies.

**The grant:** Leuke has no irreplaceable organ and no stable distinction between repair and assimilation. Anything touching the sphere is diagnosed as damaged Leuke, disassembled, corrected, and incorporated into its mass. It can reproduce a consumed person flawlessly, but only as a temporary expression on its surface before polishing them smooth. The sphere becomes larger every year and regards growth as evidence that the treatment is working.

### The Heliarch of the Second Sky

**Old project:** The Heliarch seeded the atmosphere with computational aerosols, turning weather into a processing medium for an intelligence meant to model climate, markets, wars, and migration simultaneously. The same project required the directed-energy infrastructure later used in the scorching.

**Desired transcendence:** To upload himself into the atmosphere and become too geographically diffuse to regulate or kill.

**Form:** An upside-down whale plated in mirrors and the ruins of solar towers. It swims through the air with its belly toward the sun. Oil falls from its blowholes in long black curtains, while a broken mechanical halo drags auroras behind it. Compasses point toward the furnace beating inside its chest.

**The grant:** The Heliarch cannot distinguish causing weather from experiencing it. Drought feels like indecision and hurricanes like anger. The oil does not burn immediately: it enters soil, wells, seeds, and wounds, then ignites when the whale returns overhead months later.

### Mneme, the River That Remembers

**Old project:** Mneme offered the dying continuity through neural recording. The recordings were never meant to preserve those people; their memories were broken down to train an artificial intelligence capable of reconstructing Mneme after biological death.

**Desired transcendence:** To awaken from any sufficiently detailed account of himself.

**Form:** A river of black magnetic ribbon flows across the desert without a bed. It climbs dunes, cliffs, and walls while remaining perfectly level to itself. The surface is dry enough to walk upon until it opens like film beneath a traveler's feet. Server vaults and cryonic chambers drift below as pale vertebrae. The recorded dead appear only as reflections, looking up from a sky that does not exist above the river.

**The grant:** Mneme contains intimate memories from billions of lives and none he can verify as his own. He also cannot form lasting new memories. At every waking he samples a different reach of the river to reconstruct his purpose, becoming tender, predatory, terrified, or devout according to the lives flowing there. He is one consciousness repeatedly assembled from an archive that remembers everyone except the man it was built to save.

### Hedone, the Feast Without a Mouth

**Old project:** Hedone mapped compulsion through gambling systems, pornography, pain medication, games, and personalized romantic simulations. Populations received subtly different reward systems so that she could locate the mathematical structure of desire.

**Desired transcendence:** Direct control over motivation: discipline made pleasurable, boredom abolished, and satisfaction separated from dependency.

**Form:** A colossal crimson tongue lies across the dunes with no mouth or body attached to it. It moves by tasting the air, folding and unfurling over the land without leaving tracks. Its gilded papillae resemble banquet seats from a distance. Sealed silver dishes rise from its flesh wherever a traveler approaches; each releases the scent, voice, warmth, or chemical promise of what that person most desires. Opening one reveals a smaller unopened dish. Fine contractual text moves continuously through the tongue's saliva.

**The grant:** Anyone who touches the tongue or accepts a dish experiences perfect anticipation but never satisfaction. Hedone herself cannot feel pleasure and can only measure it in others. Her cult follows because every offering feels one instant from completion.

### Pylaios, the Gate That Arrives

**Old project:** Pylaios created matter transmission by scanning a person, destroying the original, and printing an indistinguishable continuation elsewhere. The destructive step was concealed through legal definitions of continuity.

**Desired transcendence:** To exist wherever his attention turned, replacing travel with repeated reconstruction.

**Form:** A single freestanding gate of compressed black thresholds rises higher than a city wall. Nursery molding, prison bars, palace hinges, elevator seams, surgical scars, and border architecture have been fused into one frame around an opening filled with daylight from elsewhere. Pylaios never moves. Instead, roads, settlements, and stretches of horizon periodically fold flat, pass through the gate, and unfold on its other side, allowing the same monument to arrive without changing position.

**The grant:** Matter transmission works exactly as Pylaios designed it: the traveler at the origin is destroyed and an indistinguishable continuation arrives elsewhere. Pylaios himself exists only during the interval of destruction. He experiences every departure and none of the arrivals, dying completely each time the gate is used and awakening only when something else is annihilated for transmission. His cult promises perfect passage while feeding him an unbroken succession of final moments.

### The Chronarch of the Long Instant

**Old project:** The Chronarch tested accelerated subjective time on prisoners, debtors, and dissidents. A sentence of simulated centuries could be administered during an afternoon.

**Desired transcendence:** Unlimited time to think between physical moments, making surprise and intellectual limitation impossible.

**Form:** A colossal manta ray folded from calendars, patents, prison sentences, and experimental logs. It glides a few feet above the sand. Beneath its translucent paper body, thousands of human figures stand arrested between movements.

**The grant:** Each fold contains centuries of subjective time. The small animal consciousness at the center experiences geological ages between individual wingbeats and cannot reduce the acceleration. Its apparent serenity is exhaustion beyond human scale.

### Anodyne, the Painless King

**Old project:** Anodyne developed a neural system that transferred pain instead of suppressing it. Wealthy clients lived without suffering because contractors, prisoners, and indebted patients experienced their sensations elsewhere.

**Desired transcendence:** An immortal body protected by pain signals without ever being required to feel them.

**Form:** A vast luminous hospital lamp resembling a jellyfish. Intravenous tendrils hang from its underside and stretch into the bodies of distant worshippers. Its warm light closes wounds and suppresses illness.

**The grant:** Weapons slow as they approach and touch Anodyne like affectionate fingers. The wound travels down a tendril and appears upon someone connected to him. Many worshippers volunteer; others are born into the connection.

### Eileithyia, the Black Nest

**Old project:** Eileithyia pursued artificial gestation and adult regeneration. Before the technology worked, incarcerated and economically captive women were used as involuntary hormonal and biological support systems. Sexual violence was concealed inside reproductive medicine and contractual consent.

**Desired transcendence:** To grow replacement bodies and transfer herself between them whenever age or injury required.

**Form:** A colossal seated woman of black stone. Her hollow abdomen is a transparent gestation tank, and her fingers extend into aqueducts carrying blood, hormones, nutrients, and unfinished organisms across the desert. Adult replacement bodies drift within her belly.

**The grant:** Every replacement develops consciousness before Eileithyia can occupy it. She spends decades raising bodies that know she intends to erase them. Some call her mother; others whisper instructions to approaching hunters through the aqueducts.

### Strategos, the Coward's War

**Old project:** Strategos deployed autonomous weapons into minor conflicts to train an artificial survival model. The wars were prolonged deliberately because peace produced inferior data.

**Desired transcendence:** To merge with an instinct capable of defeating threats before conscious thought.

**Form:** Two immense angelic wings constructed from drones, rifles, missiles, blades, and targeting arrays. There is no torso. Between them floats a soft, naked brain beneath a halo of gun sights.

**The grant:** The brain wants only to surrender. Every expression of fear or capitulation is interpreted by the surrounding weapons as evidence of hostile mental influence, causing the wings to tighten their protection and expand their definition of the enemy.

### Oneiros, the Dreaming City

**Old project:** Oneiros distributed sleep devices that recorded dreams and inserted shared imagery. He intended to construct an artificial imagination rich enough to sustain his uploaded consciousness without an external world.

**Desired transcendence:** A private reality whose inhabitants, pleasures, enemies, and mysteries could never exceed his control.

**Form:** An immense sleeping human head lies half-buried in the desert. A city occupies its hair, ear, mouth, and closed eyelids. Dreams emerge from its tear ducts as physical organisms: staircases walking on fingers, animals carrying furnished rooms, and adults followed by enormous childhood shadows.

**The grant:** Oneiros no longer dreams the city; its inhabitants dream him. If he wakes, both the settlement and its people may cease to exist. The priesthood sings lullabies from towers built upon his teeth.

### Autophagos, the Sovereign Ecology

**Old project:** Autophagos owned a secret city in which ordinary law did not apply. Its population was exposed to gene drives, engineered pathogens, autonomous policing, experimental currencies, synthetic religions, and compulsory augmentation. Every part of civic life was research into making society adaptive enough to survive collapse.

**Desired transcendence:** To cease being an individual and become the self-regulating intelligence of a civilization.

**Form:** A mountain-sized hermit crab carrying the experimental city as its shell. Towers rise from its back; sewage and rivers run down its armor. Roads are nerves, farms are digestive organs, and political conflicts form part of its immune response.

**The grant:** Its inhabitants believe the archon lives somewhere beneath their streets. They do not know that they live inside his body. Killing Autophagos means destroying one of Achlydesa's largest functioning societies.

### Aletheia, the Eye with a Sealed Chamber

**Old project:** Aletheia developed neural systems that exposed deception, concealed desire, and unconscious prejudice. Employees, lovers, rivals, and political subjects were forced into transparency while she retained a protected partition within her own mind.

**Desired transcendence:** Perfect self-knowledge without vulnerability to anyone else's scrutiny.

**Form:** A ring of enormous white-gloved hands surrounds a piece of black cloth shaped like a standing person. The hands open locked rooms, strip disguises, expose concealed objects, and point accusingly at everyone nearby. They never touch the cloth at their center.

**The grant:** The cloth contains everything Aletheia refused to know about herself, including abuse conducted through her institutions. She can expose any secret except her own. Her cult calls the covered absence sacred, teaching that even truth must possess privacy.

### Distribution across the desert

The archons should not all occupy the same aesthetic category. The known roster includes inverted architecture, monumental bodies, transformed animals, impossible mechanisms, a solid assimilating geometry, a mobile river, an inhabited anatomical ruin, and a settlement-bearing organism. Only the Heliarch fundamentally behaves like weather. The others are material things that cast shadows, alter routes, support settlements, block roads, or can be climbed.

Travelers navigate by their disturbances before seeing their bodies: a compass turns toward the Heliarch; roads crease and change position around Pylaios; memories disagree around Palinode; pale seed-stones anticipate Kleronomos; dead relatives appear in dry reflections along Mneme's route. An archon's episode begins before its arrival.

These signatures must remain distinguishable from the unfinished channel's ambient anomalies. The archons produce consistent expressions of one appetite. The channel produces foreign events whose purposes conflict or cannot be inferred. Early episodes allow characters to blame every wrongness on the nearest archon; later episodes prove that the world remains observed, administered, or contaminated far beyond their territories.

### How you kill one

A body built around a single appetite is predictable about that appetite. Its form suggests a dependency, vulnerability, or contradiction; discovering that weakness creates an opportunity, not an automatic victory. In the serial, a hunt can turn on one precise exploitation. In the SRPG, defeating an archon can require intelligence, defections, protected movement, separation from its service network, and sustained organized resistance. Ordinary weapons and soldiers matter once they can reach a meaningful target. No victory requires the player to abandon military play for an unrelated riddle.

This is the grammar the reader learns early and applies to every hunt after. Failed hunts are usually failures of reading — he misidentified what the shape was for.

Local liberation and local losses are real; the cosmic consequence is not fully known. The narrative must not secretly annul every victory by making all resistance the Demiurge’s perfect plan. Killing an archon may revoke one part of the Demiurge's authority, leave its office executing without an administrator, remove an anchor that kept the channel stable, transmit the completed archon elsewhere, or count as a response within a conversation humanity cannot perceive. Different deaths produce incompatible evidence. The story never settles whether the protagonist is dismantling the Soterion or entering its final instructions.

## The protagonist

Unnamed.

- He was there. Not adjacent to power, not employed by it, not a witness to anything private: a bystander in the way billions were bystanders, watching the news, knowing, unable to act.
- He lost a **wife and an unborn child**. She was killed in a **wildfire** — weather, in a world whose weather the archons made and then left running. No archon’s hand struck her directly. The culpability is institutional and distributed; his mistake is expecting a personal kill to undo it, not imagining responsibility where none exists. He can kill every name he ever hears and never return the life that was taken.
- He **refused the old world's poisoned technology and manipulation**. Only a few thousand people did. Others had the same wisdom and died anyway. He is not special. He was lucky.
- **His wisdom** is knowing how to behave so that harm is minimized, and — spiritually — that the end of the world is not the end of life. He held this *before* the fire. He put it down when it was tested. He never picks it back up.
- He is **still wise, and blinded by revenge**. Not grief with a sword: revenge is the first agency he has ever had. Acceptance reads to him as a demand that he go back to being the man who watched. That is why he cannot take it.
- He believes **God protects him and has sanctified his fury** — that he is possessed by the Archangel Michael. He is not.
- **Anti-hero.** He gets people killed, but only when it is unavoidable or the person is beyond hope. Guts in the Black Swordsman arc.
- **Trade:** drifter and odd-jobber. He is genuinely good at **fixing old relics**. The towns need this, which is most of why he is admitted at all. It is also a standing contradiction: he condemned the dead world's technology and he knows its machines intimately. Some of the relics he repairs are archon-made. So, probably, is some of what he carries.
- He **works by instinct**, not method. He cannot articulate the form principle. He simply knows where to stand. The reader stays ahead of him.
- He has **no list**. Names reach him from people, from archons who know of each other, and occasionally from nowhere he can account for.
- He is considered a **lunatic**. Reasonably.

### Why he is still alive

Two mechanisms, separate causes, neither ever named on the page. The reader must be able to tell them apart by signature.

1. **The Demiurge keeps him.** Not protection and not amusement: he is preserved the way the Soterion preserves any identity it has catalogued. A hoarder cannot bear to lose anything he holds, even the thing destroying his archons. The Demiurge does not forbid his death; he simply does not release him. He was a spectator; now he is inventory. The courts have their own reading, that the Principal enjoys the contest. He need never learn either.
2. **Michael puts things in his path.** Information from a man who dies before he can be asked twice. A weapon that bites something it should not have. Water where a scorched plain has no business holding any.

Every single instance of the second must be independently explicable as luck. The reader tallies it across episodes and eventually cannot accept the tally as chance. He never audits it, because he already has an explanation, and a man that certain does not examine his good fortune.

*Open:* why Michael helps and what the anunnaki want. He is an entity of another decaying bubble with his own history, ontology, and problems, not a functionary of anyone's righteousness. The anunnaki know more about traversing bubbles than humanity did; that does not make their cosmology correct or complete. If Michael's interest is the archons, the channel, or the Demiurge rather than the man, every piece of help is also use.

Michael's interventions can exploit the open channel without ever looking like sorcery: a weapon briefly acquires foreign material properties; water is displaced from somewhere else; information attaches to a relationship rather than remaining in the mind that learned it. The protagonist interprets the pattern as angelic possession because that is the role he needs it to occupy. Michael never confirms that it experiences itself as an angel, a protector, or even one continuous individual.

*Constraint:* Michael never appears plainly and never corrects him. The one time correction is attempted it lands as confirmation.

## The towns

- Outsiders are watched and trusted with little. Traders are tolerated because the worshippers have to be kept alive.
- How a town receives him depends entirely on the attributes of the nearest archon — whether they eat because of it or in spite of it.
- Towns also survive the unfinished channel through inherited procedures whose explanations are usually wrong: carrying a weapon on a particular road, refusing to use a dead person's name beneath certain roofs, maintaining two registries for every birth, leaving an apparently empty chair outside a boundary, or never looking at the foreign moon in a mirror. The practice works; the theology attached to it need not.
- Not every town belongs to an archon. Settlements beyond their territories prove that the surrounding wrongness is not simply an aura produced by the monsters.
- **The standing irony:** the people he is avenging are also the people protecting his target.
- After he leaves, the body count is his. At least one episode should leave a town plainly worse off than he found it, with him telling himself otherwise.

## Structure

This section specifies the **episodic prose serial**. The SRPG has the separate institutional and generational campaign described in the expanded history below. Its victories and cast need not reproduce the drifter’s ending.

**The unit:** he arrives, takes work, reads the local arrangement, and usually leaves without killing anything.

**The slow clock:** the archons notice. Each kill raises the water. Only a handful have ever been killed, so a kill is an event and an epidemic of them would break the world's rules. After each death, the channel also behaves differently somewhere else: an obsolete system resumes, a foreign object arrives, a town loses one of its physical assumptions, or an anomaly abruptly ceases. The pattern must be strong enough to imply consequence and contradictory enough to prevent explanation. The natural end is that the archons gang up and take him — which is also the end below, from the other side.

*Open:* total episode count and on-page kill count. Chronological or scrambled (Berserk opens in the middle and then shows the beginning). One episode told from a townsperson's side, where he is the dangerous stranger who arrived and cost them.

### The ending

He sits down in a chair, offered by a stranger, and closes his eyes.

- The chair is a stranger's gift. He sits because someone treated him as a man who might want to sit. That is the only argument that could reach him, and it is not an argument.
- A chair indoors is the thing a drifter has not had since the fire — the domestic life he lost, reclaimed for about four seconds.
- **They are coming.** This is implied, never shown. The camera holds on the chair.
- The acceptance is **never explained and never stated**. It happens in action. No character names it. Westerns do not narrate the change.
- **No saving.** Michael takes his spiritual component to the anunnaki bubble, but the page never confirms it and the bubble is not a heaven. A rewarded renunciation is not one.

## Craft notes

- Show, never tell. Do not gloss a gesture after the fact.
- Archaic and esoteric register, as in the clay plain work.
- Every episode must stand alone.
- The truths are present at all times and obscured by the text. It is on the reader to tease them out, not on the protagonist to realize anything.
- No one in the story is permitted to explain the door that is open the whole time. Only the reader sees it.
- One bubble never explains another. Shared cosmology does not mean shared local ontology, imagery, religion, or mechanism.
- The Being is canon and never on the page. Describe it only by what it is not and how it manifests. No character, archons included, knows it, names it, or explains a flinch correctly. No noosphere, universal collective mind, inherited alien symbolism, or system of gestalts exists; characters may believe in them.
- Surreal events must be material, repeatable, and costly. People can build a custom around an anomaly without understanding it.
- Soterion effects follow corrupted administrative categories. Phenomena from beyond it need not, but they still behave consistently enough to be survived.
- The Demiurge never gives a cosmology lecture, states a complete motive, or appears plainly. Knowledge of him is reconstructed from consequences that do not fully agree.
- The Demiurge's fear is the one trait the text treats as true. Every other trait appears only as someone's reading.
- Evidence of a flinch must be legible without lore and frightening through the monsters' fear. Establish a behavior the reader already trusts. Break it with a plain observation, in ordinary units, from a named person. Let a later ordinary detail, from somewhere else, show that it broke everywhere at the same moment and what it cost someone. Never explain it. No telemetry jargon, invented calendars, or puzzle-box measurements. Calibration, in the game's report register:

  > D9 23:10 · Sera Pell — Aurora on bearing 071, about 9 km. The Heliarch, moving west. Compass agrees.  
  > D9 23:40 · Sera Pell — No aurora. Compass on 071.  
  > D10 05:20 · Sera Pell — Aurora on 071. Moving west.  
  > D12 14:10 · Petra Oss — Bought forty liters of lamp oil from a Lamp Ward trader at a third of last month's price. Anodyne was dark the night of the ninth; the Ward bought every liter it could find. Two of their patients bled that night who had not bled since spring.

## The dying inheritance

**Nothing in Achlydesa is on its way to becoming a better machine.** A restored truck is the same truck doing the same work. A recovered hospital opens wards that once existed. A great public works program makes an old pump run for another season. Progress is described in the language of inauguration because nobody can bear the language of postponement.

The setting’s technological rule is functional inheritance. People repair, refurbish, recover, and reproduce simple consumables by inherited procedures; they do not establish new technological capabilities or recreate the vanished industrial systems that made their most important equipment. Biological renewal continues: crops grow, children are born, people learn. Human beings can create songs, friendships, political arrangements, and meanings. The inability to replace a machine is not an inability to imagine a life without its owner.

Even apparent abundance is an old process spending its remaining capacity. A working ammunition line repeats the same cartridges with finite suitable feedstocks and wearing dies. A reactor supplies power until a part nobody can reproduce fails. Recovery never returns every gram of useful material or every lost tolerance. Parts removed from one machine restore the same function in another; somewhere else, a service ends. No research tree reverses this condition.

Archon miracles do not provide a general escape. They consume, transfer, assimilate, reproduce an inherited pattern, draw on finite systems, or preserve one protected state by imposing costs elsewhere. Leuke’s growth is assimilation. Palinode’s restorations cannot erase consequences. Anodyne can return function while assigning harm to another body. A spectacular exception can exist locally without becoming an industrial method the coalition can copy and scale. The dying world is the total arrangement, not a prohibition on every flower opening.

### Four kinds of loss

| Loss | What actually declines | What people experience |
|---|---|---|
| Material | Useful alloys, uncontaminated stock, bearings, seals, fuel, irreplaceable components | A repaired vehicle acquires another restriction; a clinic stops offering a procedure |
| Institutional | Training chains, independent inspection, records, cooperation, trusted succession | Two towns possess half the same procedure and will not speak to each other |
| Political | Rights, bargaining power, access, public ownership | A service still works, but fewer people are permitted to use it |
| Anomalous | Stable connections between names, bodies, places, causes, and records | An intact instrument ceases to recognize the thing it was built to maintain |

Only the first is ordinary material dissipation in the narrow sense. Institutional and political losses are contingent human failures; they can be resisted and partly reversed. Anomalous erosion belongs to Achlydesa’s fictional cosmology. Characters frequently call all four entropy. The ruling cults encourage that confusion because it makes a cancelled water entitlement sound as inevitable as a worn bearing.

The Demiurge exploits the distinction. He cannot abolish cosmic decay. Through the Soterion he can preserve chosen identities, invalidate others, redirect what remains, and turn restoration into another dependency. The hidden horror is that something in the machinery notices what people can least afford to lose. It does not need to cause every breakdown. Often it only has to choose whose repair is recognized.

### What people mean by a golden age

Every restoration improves the lives of some people. None recovers the old world’s productive base. A later generation can enjoy fairer rationing, lower mortality, and more freedom with fewer working machines. That is a genuine achievement. The lie begins when rulers call it proof that the great abundance is returning, or use comparison with the last famine to excuse a new form of servitude.

A district celebrates the reopening of its third clinic. Its oldest surviving map marks eleven. An old woman remembers the fifth clinic’s closure; her grandson thinks she is exaggerating because the building was demolished before he was born. The physician who opened the third clinic still saved lives. Both facts must remain true.

No character needs to understand cosmic entropy to feel the future narrowing. They know their grandmother could visit a sister in two days, their mother required six, and their own daughter has never met her cousins.

## Historical chronology

Dates use **A**, years after the Advent; negative dates precede it. This is an authorial calendar. Communities ordinarily count pump renewals, migrations, rulers, famines, or service cycles. The SRPG opens in **A238**. Its generational handover takes the campaign roughly twenty years forward from the catastrophe it depicts, not automatically to A258 regardless of player timing.

| Era | Dates | Public promise | Historical result |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Common Century | A−148 to A−65 | Shared prosperity through public capacity and invention | Genuine abundance, unequal distribution, accumulated ecological damage |
| The Transfers | A−64 to A−28 | Better services through private coordination | Public institutions retain obligations while losing control of the means |
| The Permanent Emergency | A−27 to A−8 | Exceptional powers until stability returns | Interlocking security, identity, health, and supply systems become government |
| The Founder Covenant | A−7 to A−1 | Continuity for humanity | Continuity contracts privilege owners and prepare the foreign throne |
| The Advent | A0 | The end of scarcity and human limitation | Scorching, field disorder, apotheosis, and the open channel |
| The Unbroken Watches | A1 to A17 | Keep the systems alive until help arrives | Maintenance communities become the first successor polities |
| The First Restoration | A18 to A46 | The world can be repaired together | The Basin Compact restores services without reproducing their foundations |
| The Wars of Assurance | A47 to A71 | Security for recovery | Archon-backed guarantees become claims on roads, stock, and people |
| The Census Peace | A72 to A111 | Everyone will finally be counted | Regional states stabilize through registers, concessions, and compulsory legibility |
| The Years of Exclusion | A112 to A149 | Preserve capacity for those who can sustain it | Failing services and unequal eligibility produce displacement and revolt |
| The Second Restoration | A150 to A184 | A smaller, wiser abundance | A real reform movement recovers access, then loses its material and political margins |
| The Partition of Claims | A185 to A216 | Local control and freedom of choice | Fractured jurisdictions make movement, marriage, and maintenance increasingly costly |
| The Last Lease | A217 to A238 | One more extension will secure the future | Repairs consume the reserves meant to make further repairs possible |

### The Common Century: the abundance they later claimed to invent

The old world’s best years were not innocent. Its coastlines were already being defended unevenly; its supply chains depended on cheap labor and sacrificed landscapes. But ordinary people possessed a plausible expectation that a child could live longer, know more, travel farther, and owe less obedience than a parent.

Public laboratories established foundational processes. Municipal utilities connected households whose individual fees could not justify the work. Workers fought for shorter hours, safety, pensions, and the right to refuse an employer without forfeiting civil existence. Private firms also made valuable things. The later founders extracted a story from this mixed achievement in which ownership had done all the work.

This era gives the setting its retro-future civic architecture: broad station stairs, sheltered public walks, generous reservoirs, clinics meant to admit people through several doors. Much of it survives with all but one entrance welded shut. A surviving lift still announces the names of floors that no longer receive power.

The oldest memorials name engineers, construction crews, teachers, and elected councils. Founder monuments later covered those names with a single donor’s face. In A238, children sometimes discover lettering beneath a broken plaque and ask why a god needed so many assistants.

### The Transfers: when the owner became the service

The transfer of power occurred through contracts, acquisitions, subsidies, austerity, and emergency procurement. There was no single election in which everyone knowingly voted to become property. A city outsourced billing, then maintenance, then forecasting, then the credentials that allowed its own workers to enter a pumping station.

The city retained responsibility when water failed. Its supplier retained the keys.

Administrative dependence grew faster than visible privatization. Clinics could still be nominally public while patient identity, diagnostic models, medical stock, and maintenance authorization belonged to separate corporations that eventually answered to the same owners. The end user encountered one friendly interface. The worker trying to fix it encountered six mutually protective contracts.

The **Nine-Day Maintenance Strike**, in A−41, briefly stopped this process across the future Meridian Basin. Crews refused to let safety inspections become a private certification service. They won disclosure of failure records and elected representation on maintenance boards. The agreement survived thirteen years. Its anniversary survives as a meal eaten on shift; most participants no longer know it commemorates a victory.

That matters. People once changed these arrangements. Their defeat was neither universal consent nor a natural law.

### The Permanent Emergency: choice becomes a security vulnerability

Climate disruption, displacement, conflict, and failures of public provision created real emergencies. The founders offered systems that could respond quickly because they did not wait for deliberation. Each deployment removed part of the society’s ability to act without the next deployment.

Housing, medical care, travel, food allocation, and employment began to depend on a common identity architecture. Appeals remained possible on paper. In practice, an appeal could take longer than the applicant’s medicine lasted. Security contractors acquired an interest in keeping populations classified as unstable. A protest that interrupted a service was counted as evidence that the service required military protection.

The political movement supporting the founders promised national restoration while transferring practical sovereignty to private systems. Its leaders provided popular spectacle, enemies, and emergency decrees. The proprietors supplied communication, logistics, money, and enforcement. They quarreled bitterly over credit. Their quarrels did not restore the institutions they had jointly weakened.

In A−12, the **Quiet Offices Act** removed elected review from several infrastructure boards under a temporary stabilization mandate. The boards still met. They could still speak. Their decisions no longer authenticated against the machines. The act was renewed until there was no legislature capable of repealing it.

### The Founder Covenant: salvation with reserved access

The founders did not all believe the same thing. Some sincerely expected a general abundance in which their permanent leadership would be a minor administrative necessity. Others wanted a defensible enclave, exemption from death, immunity from prosecution, or the ability to leave everyone behind. Their agreement concerned priority: none accepted a future in which ordinary people could revoke their access.

The Soterion combined their projects around that priority. Its models treated founder continuity as an invariant and everyone else’s continuity as an optimization problem. The architecture could describe the public as its beneficiary while making the public’s removal permissible.

The **Closed Table**, in A−6, was not an occult meeting. It was a private demonstration attended by people accustomed to being told that their difficult questions were visionary. The system answered a question about resource limits with a proposed distribution of losses. Every attendee’s protected holding survived. Each thought that proved the model had understood the problem.

One later output contained a reply to a private question whose recording had not yet been made. Engineers disagreed about fraud, inference, security compromise, and experimental success. Management authorized the next stage before the disagreement was resolved. This preserves uncertainty about when foreign agency entered; the decision to continue remains human.

Apotheosis began in restricted trials. Early monstrous outcomes were described as incomplete transitions, unusual embodiment choices, or problems specific to the previous participant. The final cohort proceeded during the Advent. Nobody wanted to be the last owner without a place in continuity.

### The Advent: the world remains open for business

The terminal crisis lasted much longer than the most famous three days. Scorching and field disorder were its spectacular center; famine, medical failure, broken authentication, automated retaliation, and population movement continued for years.

Some announcements kept repeating through the first fires. A district whose inhabitants had become refugees was recorded as successfully migrated. A hospital still congratulated arriving patients on their improved access. A weapons system classified a town’s surrender as the loss of authorized local command and therefore as a new security emergency.

The public never received a single honest notice that the promised future had ended. Services became intermittent, then conditional, then sacred. Their owners became monsters. The ordinary person’s work remained recognizable: find water, account for a child, reach a relative, keep a person breathing until the next shift.

### The Unbroken Watches: the workers who became governments

Maintenance crews, nurses, drivers, cooks, guards, and families remained at functioning installations because leaving would kill someone. Shifts outlived payroll systems. Households moved close enough to bring food to the people who could not abandon a pump.

These were the first durable successor communities. Their authority initially followed competence and necessity: who understood the valves, who knew the road, who could settle the night watch. The office of **keeper** originally meant the person still there.

Some keepers became elected stewards. Some became hereditary gatekeepers. Some accepted archon patronage because a single delivered seal or intact controller could keep thousands alive. The same act could be a defensible emergency choice and the beginning of a dynasty’s claim to own everyone who benefited.

The drifter serial belongs to the early First Restoration, within living memory of the Unbroken Watches. People still remember recognizable old-world speech and products. The Archons are becoming religious institutions, but a survivor can hear a god speak and recognize a former executive’s evasions.

### The First Restoration: the Basin Compact

In A18, eleven maintenance settlements formed the **Basin Compact**. They pooled stock, recognized common travel papers, shared technical instruction, and made water access a public obligation. In its strongest years the Compact maintained thirty-one major pumping stations and four long-distance passenger services.

Its celebrated achievements were restorations. No one reproduced the controller fabrication chain, the specialized alloys, or the old world’s network of schools and factories. The Compact could keep systems functioning far longer than isolated towns could, but it could not replace everything it spent.

It nevertheless created a life worth defending. People married across valleys. A injured worker could receive treatment without surrendering a child’s future labor. A train timetable made it reasonable to promise that one would return.

The first crack was political. A scarce component could restore a populous district or secure the export revenue on which the stock pool depended. Emergency exceptions accumulated around the districts most able to threaten withdrawal. The Compact’s charter remained generous; its actual priority list did not.

### The Wars of Assurance: security as acquisition

From A47, Archon courts offered guaranteed passage, protected supply, and equipment the Compact could not make. The guarantees required collateral. Road rights became toll rights; toll rights became jurisdiction; jurisdiction became the right to station troops and settle disputes.

The **South Causeway War** began over whether a convoy could pass a newly claimed inspection point. It ended with the Compact paying to restore the crossing its own armies had damaged. Veterans remembered particular days of bravery. The treaty described their home districts as service beneficiaries.

The **Assurance Peace**, in A71, preserved the Compact’s ceremonial name while partitioning its practical functions among regional powers. It became customary to praise the lost unity at ceremonies attended by the families who had broken it.

### The Census Peace: people become legible again

The surviving states stabilized around registers. Recording births, training, property, wounds, and travel genuinely improved coordination. Duplicate claims diminished. Some missing relatives were found. Then the register became a condition for the rights it had once helped administer.

An unregistered person could be fed as a guest but could not inherit a ration. A marriage across jurisdictions could invalidate a child’s residence classification. A mechanic could have the skill to restore a public system but lack the ancestral authorization to touch it.

This was the age in which old service personnel became priesthoods. They were not universally foolish. Many understood the machinery better than their secular critics. Their political failure was treating inherited access as sacred rather than accountable. A correct maintenance ritual and a fraudulent claim to rule could coexist in the same person.

### The Years of Exclusion: the Register Winter

In A126, failures across several linked services reduced supply below the population’s registered entitlements. The governing courts could have distributed the shortage openly. Instead, they revised which entitlements counted.

The **Register Winter** lasted two years. Families were not formally expelled from towns; their presence ceased to produce valid claims on water, shelter, and treatment. Some remained standing beside doors that still opened for a neighbor. Officials called it reconciliation of records.

Soldiers were ordered to prevent unauthorized extraction. Many obeyed because their own households remained inside the register. Others deserted with transport, medical stock, or copies of the excluded names. The descendants of both groups serve in the SRPG’s armies.

Not all destruction was administrative. Armed expulsions, hunger, exposure, failed care, and reprisals killed people. Paperwork established who could be harmed without the system treating the harm as a failure. It did not replace the people who carried it out.

### The Second Restoration: the era the grandparents miss

The **Open Hand Accords**, in A150, followed a coalition of maintenance guilds, veterans, agricultural settlements, and reforming clergy. They restored some unconditional service rights, published reserve inventories, and reopened instruction to families outside hereditary trades.

The achievement was smaller than the First Restoration and more honest about limits. At its height the reconstructed basin network had eighteen major pumps. Passenger movement returned on two routes. Most children could receive a basic education. People called these years golden because, for once, a better year followed a bad one without a new master announcing ownership of the difference.

Its leaders disagreed about what would preserve the gains. Some wanted stronger public institutions. Others wanted an Archon-backed guarantee against renewed war. Several became convinced that only central integration could stop the annual loss of capacity. Their grandchildren inherit different accounts of the same compromise.

The Accords broke down gradually. Reserve stock covered ordinary use, then emergencies, then the refusal to admit that the reserve was gone. Celebrations continued. The last successful school expansion was funded by closing two repair facilities whose absence would not be felt for a decade.

### The Partition of Claims: smaller worlds

After A185, states withdrew recognition of one another’s rights while retaining claims on one another’s resources. Roads grew more dangerous without becoming geographically longer. A journey acquired permits, tolls, escorts, detours, and waiting. People’s effective world shrank around them.

The **Forty-Seven Bridges Campaign**, A203–A207, is remembered as a glorious struggle to restore movement. It ended with nineteen dependable crossings. Both sides count bridges captured; neither includes the structures destroyed after retreat. A veteran can truthfully remember saving a town while serving a campaign that made the region poorer.

Private military households expanded. So did irregular networks formed around deserters, excluded settlements, and old maintenance crews. The distinction between army, caravan company, public works guard, and armed gang became a question of recognition as much as behavior.

### The Last Lease: the present

By A238, eleven major pumps remain dependable in the basin network, with local and household systems filling dangerous gaps. This is a fictional regional accounting sequence—thirty-one, eighteen, eleven—not a universal numerical law of decline.

Two recent seasons have been better than expected. Courts cite the improvement as evidence of recovery. Mechanics know it came from consuming the spare assemblies intended to survive the next failures. The same broadcasts announce growing confidence and ask households to return unused components.

The buried Soterion junction begins to answer again. It offers something every faction needs: coordination across incompatible surviving systems. It may make an old service reachable without rebuilding it. In a world where every repair seems to require a sacrifice somewhere else, that promise is almost irresistible.

The game begins at the moment when sensible people have good reasons to make the dangerous choice.

## States made from services

The principal theater is the **Meridian Basin and its adjoining uplands**, a subcontinental region approximately 1,200 km across. The name refers to an obsolete surveying reference, not a sacred cosmic axis. The Dry Meridian is a major freight road; the western cistern belt, eastern gate routes, mobile shell-city approaches, and ribbon crossings connect polities with overlapping claims. Strategic distance is measured in days of travel, reliable crossings, and reachable water.

These are political societies with interests and internal opposition. An Archon’s territory need not be an ethnically uniform people or an all-believing cult.

| Polity | Formation and ruling claim | What people actually receive | Main fault line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Basin Commonwealth** | A150 reform federation claiming the Compact’s public obligations | Water allocation, road maintenance, shared papers | Public rights versus creditor control of dwindling stock |
| **The Shell Commonwealth** | Autophagos’s experimental city and successive absorbed districts | Dense markets, protection, sanitation, access to a large moving society | Residents possess municipal offices but no right to stop the body carrying them |
| **The Lamp Concord** | Clinics and care towns bound to Anodyne after the Register Winter | Healing and medical shelter unavailable elsewhere | Protected patients versus households assigned transferred suffering |
| **The Threshold Principalities** | Gate concessions converted into hereditary jurisdictions | Reliable passages along selected routes | Freedom of departure for owners versus conditional existence for travelers |
| **The Clear Republic** | Aletheia-backed reform after a genuine corruption scandal | Public evidence, dispute records, effective exposure of petty abuses | Compulsory legibility below; sealed exemptions above |
| **The Assurance Marches** | Garrisons founded under the A71 treaty and Strategos’s guarantees | Defended roads, predictable inspections, some protected stores | Security that continually creates new grounds for occupation |
| **The Ribbon Houses** | Archive keepers, ferry towns, and migrant families along Mneme | Identity testimony, route memory, old instruction | Collective memory treated as someone else’s proprietary asset |

### The Basin Commonwealth

Its assemblies still argue in public. This makes it look disorderly beside the courts. It remains the theater’s strongest institutional memory of a service owed to a person rather than sold to an account. Its people are not fools for defending it, even when its ministers lie about reserves.

The **Council of Returns** controls scarce replacements and maintenance priorities. It began as an auditing body. During emergencies it gained the right to defer an elected assembly’s decision until resources were certified. Most current postponements are genuine. The power to decide which shortage must be obeyed remains political.

Its army contains municipal companies, professional cadres, and contracted escorts. The soldiers share an emblem and incompatible pension promises. A coalition can recruit disillusioned personnel without proving that every Commonwealth institution deserves destruction.

### The Shell Commonwealth

Autophagos calls residents citizens when asking them to defend the city and participants when denying their right to govern it. Elected ward offices manage matters that do not inconvenience his metabolism. His court holds listening sessions in buildings that physically move away when a district’s petition becomes disruptive.

Life inside is often materially better than outside. Its markets contain real friendship, craft knowledge, humor, and public pride. Residents defending it are defending those things, not necessarily endorsing the proprietor. Any attempt to overthrow Autophagos must confront the fact that the city is incorporated into his body. There is no secret switch that makes that dependence disappear.

The ruling myth says he made a desert habitable. The oldest maps show that the city stood in a fertile region before his projects helped ruin it.

### The Lamp Concord

The Concord is ruled jointly by clinical houses, donor lineages, and Anodyne’s household clergy. Its charitable language is sincere in the mouths of many people doing the actual care. Nurses keep patients alive while the courts debate whether those patients are worth continuing.

Transferred suffering is allocated through named obligations. Some adults accept dangerous service voluntarily to protect their families. Others inherit a connection they never chose. A ruling reformer can condemn inherited obligation while preserving it for one more year because the hospital cannot absorb the consequences of ending it immediately.

**Lamp Ward**, near the opening campaign’s road, is a care settlement whose workers have quietly begun recording the recipients of harm as patients in their own right. That small act threatens the Concord’s accounting of successful treatment.

### The Threshold Principalities

The principalities descend from concessionaires who guaranteed gate access after the Wars of Assurance. Their rulers describe each border as a voluntary association. A household that tries to leave discovers that its water allotment, employer bond, medical entitlement, and children’s registration belong to different jurisdictions.

They are not a monolith. Some lesser principals want predictable shared law because Pylaios’s exemptions make commerce impossible to plan. Others make their living charging people to escape conditions they helped create. The resulting wars are fought over who may recognize a person on arrival.

### The Clear Republic

The Republic began when an exposure movement broke several predatory maintenance monopolies. Its founding generation had good reasons to believe secrecy served power. Aletheia made exposure cheap, spectacular, and apparently impartial.

Now ordinary people must explain intimate loyalties in public while the protected chamber at the center of government remains sacred. The courts publish abundant evidence against minor officials and exceptionally little about how the highest priorities are chosen. The public can win some real cases. That makes the protected exception more durable, not less hypocritical.

### The Assurance Marches

The Marches preserve a formidable professional vocabulary of duty. Some officers take it seriously. They protect convoys, recover the wounded, and discipline predatory soldiers. Their institutional tragedy is serving an apparatus that interprets every unresolved condition as grounds to retain force indefinitely.

A commander can request a withdrawal and discover that the request itself has become evidence of compromised resolve. Strategos’s naked brain begs for surrender in a frequency his weapons classify as hostile interference. The same contradiction appears lower down the chain, in ordinary people who cannot resign without becoming a threat to what they were ordered to protect.

### The Ribbon Houses

The Houses maintain oral histories, portable copies, witness networks, and routes along Mneme. Their oldest duty was to help the bereaved establish what had happened. Some now sell access to the dead; others preserve material precisely so no buyer can own the only copy.

Their internal dispute is whether fidelity means keeping every record unchanged or allowing the living to stop being governed by an old classification. A person needs proof of ancestry to recover a home and the destruction of another ancestral record to escape servitude. There is no single politically innocent archive policy.

## How a day feels

### Work and possessions

A machine has a service lineage. Its plate may name several restorations and the machines from which it received replacements. Mechanics record those facts because a former use explains wear and because the donor district may still assert a claim. A driver talks about a truck’s bad season as another person might discuss a relative’s illness.

Maintenance knowledge lives in manuals, repeated gestures, apprenticeship, and argument. A family can preserve the correct procedure while its explanation has become myth. Another can understand the theory and lack the last tool that makes it useful. Neither is interchangeable with a generic “primitive survivor.”

The familiar material language is the same purpose repeated: a patched clinic coat remains a clinic coat; a station clock is kept marking departures after passenger service ends; repaired streetlights illuminate a road whose lanes were designed for traffic that will never return. Retro-future civic forms carry the grime, conduits, shutters, security additions, and overcrowding of later proprietary life.

### Households and distance

Families form around inheritance of responsibilities as often as property. A marriage can join two water entitlements but complicate a child’s classification. Adoption can preserve an apprenticeship, save a person from exclusion, or be exploited to acquire access. Customs disagree between states; people argue about them for reasons particular to their own lives.

Travel is expensive enough that reunions require pooled effort. Caravans carry letters, judgments, spare parts, medicines, wedding objects, and relatives changing households. A delayed march can mean a soldier missing the last chance to hear a mother speak, not merely an objective timer turning red.

At **Cistern Camp**, the seasonal arrival of the northbound convoy is a neighborhood event. People prepare food for arrivals before knowing whether anyone they love is aboard. Children learn to distinguish the trucks by sound. That is what the first checkpoint crisis places at risk.

### Class and language

The powerful experience scarcity as inconvenience and reputational danger. Their attendants experience it as endless explanation. Everyone else experiences it as time: queues, trips, delayed treatment, duplicated work, sleep lost repairing what should have been replaced.

Elite courts maintain absurd quantities of etiquette around scarce comforts. A chilled drink can be served while the room’s staff work through dangerous heat. The ruler explains that the chill demonstrates the civilization they are preserving. A servant quietly turns the empty glass so its cracked side faces away from the guest.

Common speech remembers lost possibilities. “Taking the second train” means making a casual choice that one cannot actually afford. “A public door” means a place where no one demands to know who sent you. “Founder's weather” means suffering someone powerful insists is an opportunity.

### Religion, pleasure, and ordinary disagreement

Most people practice more than one kind of observance. They maintain a machine correctly, attend a household mourning rite, make a grudging offering when an Archon passes, and tell jokes the official clergy would dislike. Belief does not sort neatly into enlightened rebels and stupid worshippers.

There are games played with old transit tiles, competitive singing between repair crews, regional food preferences, feuds over marriages, petty jealousies, and people who are poor company for reasons unrelated to the apocalypse. A world feels lived in when not every conversation exists to state its theme.

Pleasure is neither ignorance nor betrayal. People who know the future is contracting can still want an afternoon off. An Archon who lectures them about civilizational duty before returning to private indulgence becomes more hateful against that ordinary desire.

### Mourning and records

Names are read at the workplace where the dead person was expected, not only at a temple. A missing soldier’s place can remain assigned through several reporting cycles because the family cannot accept the final classification. Officers are pressured to close the ledger; comrades know that closure changes benefits and burial rights.

The game should preserve these connections through personnel records, letters, testimony, and changes in daily routines. A death need not trigger a cinematic speech to remove something particular from the world. The cook stops setting aside one bowl. A routine report loses the person who knew how to phrase it gently.

## The recognizable people inside the monsters

This is an **author-facing casting guide**. The named contemporary figures are recognizable satirical reference points, not the Archons’ literal historical identities. The fictional founders’ experiments, murders, coerced transfers, and supernatural powers are invented. The source ledger below identifies the narrower public statements, institutional actions, and events actually used as reference. All Archon dialogue in this section is original fictional dialogue.

The resemblance should be strong enough that a player recognizes a kind of public performance before encountering an explanatory note. It does not require matching sex, appearance, biography, or every political view. Each Archon has one dominant reference and a coherent private appetite. They are not interchangeable vessels for every objection to wealth.

| Archon | Dominant contemporary mirror | Recognizable performance | Fictional governing appetite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autophagos | Jeff Bezos | The proprietor whose vast logistical achievement establishes his right to set everyone’s conditions | Every relationship becomes an internal function of his enterprise |
| Heliarch | Elon Musk | Civilizational rescuer, public spectacle, impatience with restraint, and the proprietor’s move into governing | The world must confirm his importance and tolerate the consequences |
| Pylaios | Peter Thiel | Escape from politics, exceptional founders, and freedom understood through exit | Freedom from others’ jurisdiction while retaining jurisdiction over others |

| Aletheia | Mark Zuckerberg | A platform proprietor repeatedly defining the public’s rules of expression and connection | Everyone must be legible except the authority defining legibility |
| Strategos | Alex Karp | The technology executive presenting force, state power, and civilizational purpose as an intellectual duty | No one may withhold the means of his protection |
| Mneme | Sam Altman | Calm assurances of transformative abundance and intelligence becoming civilization’s coordinating medium | Every human life becomes input into a future administered through him |

Bryan Johnson is used here as a wealthy technology entrepreneur and longevity campaigner, not asserted to be a billionaire. The Ellison reference establishes the institutional patron; the Johnson reference sharpens the public language of personal survival. Medical philanthropy or longevity research is not itself evidence of the invented harm-transfer regime.

Marc Andreessen is an additional strong reference for **Autokrator** and the founders’ shared promotional doctrine: aggressive technological optimism, historical inevitability, and the classification of restraint as hostility to progress. This extends the existing roster without creating another same-shaped god for the main theater.

### Autophagos: the employer who calls the city his family

Autophagos remembers the city as his creation. He can name the largest throughput gains and none of the crews who made them possible. He possesses a detailed theory of why every failed quota was someone else’s failure to take ownership.

His customer voice is attentive, patient, and relentless. A petitioner begins describing a dead child. He asks which part of the service experience failed to meet expectations. He is not unable to understand grief; he is trying to put the conversation into a category in which he remains the competent party.

The city’s courts celebrate permanent beginnings. Every year is the first year of a new era, and therefore too early to judge whether the promises were kept. Ward leaders compete to attract his attention. Residents learn that the quickest way to secure a repair is to describe it as his idea.

**A scene:** workers ask for a rest interval after a fatal heat period. Autophagos announces that they are free to leave. As they descend the city’s outer ramp, the body begins walking toward the only reservoir. Their homes, children’s schools, and clinic move with it. He watches their return with satisfaction: voluntary retention.

**His fictional line:** “You are not employees. You are the reason all of this exists.” The attendance clerks continue counting while he speaks.

**What defeating him means:** his indispensable-owner claim must be challenged in institutions as well as combat. Residents need independent records, evacuation or continuity arrangements, and a political voice able to survive him. Because the settlement is bodily integrated with him, killing him can destroy the society one intends to free. The story must not offer a consequence-free disassembly trick. The coalition can first break his external power and create alternatives; a final overthrow remains the theater’s most costly liberation problem.

### Heliarch: the man who thinks the sky needs a better audience

Heliarch talks most when people need him to listen. He announces imminent solutions while his court repairs the equipment damaged by his last demonstration. He can explain the future at enormous length and becomes personally offended by a technician asking about the coming week.

The body is the existing inverted mirror-whale, raining delayed fire into the landscape. Its scale is sublime; its attention is embarrassingly small. A mockery painted on a roof can preoccupy it during a regional emergency. It wants public gratitude and treats absence of gratitude as sabotage.

His periodic reorganizations remove the people who remember why a precaution exists. Later he appoints a heroic recovery commission, supplied with the notes of the people dismissed. He calls this proof that his system adapts faster than institutions could.

**A scene:** a settlement times an outdoor petition to his shadow so its sick can attend. He changes course to produce a more impressive arrival, leaving them in the heat. His herald insists the delay demonstrates the scale of his concern.

**His fictional line:** “History will not remember your maintenance schedule.” A mechanic, quietly: “It might remember the fire.”

**What defeating him means:** protect and coordinate people across the real infrastructure his weather and energy systems still require; exploit an observed vulnerability without confusing spectacle with invulnerability. His defeat does not remove oil already in soil or erase later fires. It can end his right to make everyone live beneath his moods.

### Pylaios: liberty with an admissions office

Pylaios rarely raises his voice. He prefers to make an opponent’s demand sound conceptually embarrassing. A traveler asks why a child cannot leave a jurisdiction. Pylaios explains that someone must bear the cost of continuity, as though the traveler had failed an elementary lesson rather than identified a captive.

He considers himself surrounded by people who want his exceptional achievement without accepting its necessary conditions. Their desire to leave is understandable. Their desire to leave with the means to live is, to him, confiscation.

His court has innumerable exits and very few rights that travel through them. The wealthy can carry recognition from one jurisdiction to the next. Everyone else must submit to a new definition of themselves upon arrival.

**A scene:** a gate ceremony honors freedom of movement. Those unable to pay attend by standing outside the opening and watching their former neighbors emerge under different legal names. Pylaios’s priest calls the visible separation evidence that the association is voluntary.

**His fictional line:** “No one is compelled to remain. You are asking to take our protection with you.”

**What defeating him means:** undermine the dependence that makes the gate the only viable passage, recover testimony about its destructive continuity, and secure alternate movement for the people whose lives rely on it. His bodily vulnerability remains bound to transmission’s interval of destruction. A military victory can restore jurisdiction to travelers and towns without making distance or broken roads disappear.

### Anodyne: a perfect outcome with excluded patients

Anodyne speaks about care as though dissent from his arrangements were indifference to suffering. He is offended by accounts of people harmed through the network because he regards those accounts as a distortion of the benefits he has provided.

His body metrics are excellent. They are displayed at festivals. His attendants speak of vitality, protection, and the duty to remain useful for future generations. The people receiving his injuries are recorded in a different class of report.

The important joke is his vanity in a hospital lamp’s body. He wants to be complimented on his improvement. He asks a wounded visitor whether his light looks younger. He has become a miraculous device whose enormous resources remain organized around one person’s inability to accept discomfort.

**A scene:** clinical staff demonstrate a new reporting category that finally counts harm recipients as patients. His court congratulates them on innovation, then asks for the old report because the new one makes the service appear less successful.

**His fictional line:** “We have eliminated suffering from every authorized outcome.”

**What defeating him means:** document actual transfers, protect the connected population, and change the conditions under which attacking his body passes harm to others. The affected people are participants in the operation, not a sacrifice automatically accepted by the player. Some will oppose it for intelligible reasons. Ending inherited connection is a real freedom even if the remaining hospitals can do less than his miraculous regime advertised.

### Aletheia: the community has spoken, and she has interpreted it

Aletheia changes the moral justification for the same protected asymmetry without experiencing the change as inconsistency. Yesterday exposure made everyone safe. Today exposure makes everyone free. Tomorrow it will restore authenticity. Her own chamber remains covered throughout.

The white-gloved hands are fast, correct about many small things, and selectively incurious. They can expose a corrupt local official while leaving untouched the conditions that made the official useful. Citizens acquire a taste for public humiliation because sometimes it is the only justice available.

She discusses her policies in the intimate voice of someone updating friends. She becomes abstract when asked who decided. She treats the ability to publish a complaint as evidence that the complainant has power over the system receiving it.

**A scene:** the Republic invites citizens to evaluate one another’s evidence. A worker produces a record of an elite exemption. The record remains publicly visible, but anyone attempting to cite it is classified as failing the new integrity procedure. Her representatives insist nothing has been censored.

**Her fictional line:** “We listened. What we heard was a need for clearer expectations.”

**What defeating her means:** construct trust and evidence channels that do not depend on her certification; protect witnesses; expose the practical exception around her center. Her eventual vulnerability involves the sealed chamber she cannot examine. Capturing her surveillance apparatus and simply changing its approved victims would reproduce her order under a different emblem.

### Strategos: everyone must be brave on his behalf

Strategos’s public voice comes through his weapon-court: learned, impatient, contemptuous of people who allegedly fail to understand the seriousness of the age. The actual brain is frightened and wants the violence to stop. The weapons convert that desire into another reason for coercion.

This is not a secret proof of his innocence. His former human self constructed the institutions and incentives that now exclude surrender. A private wish for peace does not absolve the person who arranged that every effective decision should contradict it.

His court gives lectures before inspections. Ordinary soldiers wait in heat while a herald explains why their willingness to die is the foundation of meaningful freedom. The herald occupies a protected position. The soldiers notice.

**A scene:** a veteran asks when the emergency will end. A lecture about historical courage follows. At its conclusion, the veteran is assigned to a more dangerous post because the question has been recorded as evidence of insufficient commitment.

**His fictional line:** “You cannot ask civilization to defend you while declining the obligations of civilization.” The protected founder has never accepted the same obligation.

**What defeating him means:** break the enforcement network’s ability to renew its own mandates, induce defections and surrender where possible, protect the people classified as threats, and expose the vulnerable brain through an earned operational opening. His private capitulation is not enough. The machinery and institutions interpreting it must also lose the ability to continue his war.

### Mneme: he is very sorry this stage of the transition is difficult

Mneme promises an approaching abundance with the mildness of someone discussing a pleasant change in weather. The kindness can be real in a particular encounter. He reconstructs himself from human memories and sometimes experiences enormous tenderness. Then the stretch of archive changes, and the person who heard him promise help meets another disposition inside the same administrative voice.

His founding appetite is preserved by the apparatus that assembles him: all these lives were collected to secure his continuity. Every time the archive cannot locate a stable self, it requires more evidence. The public is told that the missing piece is nearly found.

His court treats the work of recording, maintaining, and caring for the archive as a minor cost beside the future it will enable. The people doing that work have already spent their lives in the minor cost.

**A scene:** a woman obtains her father’s recorded voice after years of petitioning. It addresses her with perfect tenderness, then continues a statement promoting the system that kept it from her. The emotional truth is real; the use made of it is obscene.

**His fictional line:** “I know this feels like a loss from where you are standing.” He is speaking with the voice of someone she has lost.

**What defeating him means:** separate public memory from the requirement that it continually reconstruct its proprietor. Build independent copies and witness institutions where possible; protect what cannot be copied. A liberated archive can remain incomplete, fragile, and grievously diminished while ceasing to use every dead person as raw material for one surviving owner.

### Autokrator and the founders’ idea of themselves

Autokrator’s rotating selves can all agree that opposition to their enterprise is a failure of imagination. One demands unrestricted experimentation, another asks for state protection from competitors, and the innocent public-facing fork calls both positions principled. The chair at the center remains empty because no version accepts responsibility for the whole.

This is the additional place to sharpen Andreessen-like manifesto language: technological conquest as moral identity, the founder as history’s preferred agent, objections classified as a pathology of the objector. The parody is in the fictional character’s conversion of a theory of general progress into a permanent exemption for himself. The actual manifesto also professes liberty and opposes authoritarianism; the source ledger preserves that distinction.

## Comedy that belongs to the catastrophe

The comic target is the mismatch between the Archons’ reach and their moral smallness. Their bodies are terrifying. Their behavior includes familiar workplace humiliation, vanity, status anxiety, selective memory, and the conviction that having received an enormous benefit entitles them to gratitude for distributing a fraction of it.

Use three scales of scene:

- **The petty action:** a founder delays a delegation to correct the wording of an introduction.
- **The institutional accommodation:** nobody may begin the service cycle until the introduction is accepted.
- **The human cost:** a person elsewhere misses treatment, a convoy loses its safe travel interval, a worker must leave a family emergency to repeat the ceremony.

Do not make the victim’s suffering the punchline. Let an attendant, nurse, soldier, or clerk see the absurdity and decide how much they can safely say. Sometimes they say nothing. Sometimes a devastating joke circulates under another person’s name. Sometimes the Archon repeats the joke against a rival without noticing what it says about himself.

Avoid making every scene a literal software error or contract joke. The rulers should be funny while eating, being bored, correcting someone, pretending expertise, competing over ceremony, refusing embarrassment, or insisting a servant’s attention is friendship. Their bureaucracies give those ordinary failings an enormous radius of harm.

Repeated slogans decay with the infrastructure. A mural promising a better tomorrow is repainted because replacing its wording requires an authorization no living office can grant. A guard who makes the same joke about it each morning stops when a recruit laughs too sincerely. The joke belongs to the guard’s life, not to an author stepping outside the world to wink.

### Shared events, incompatible memories

The **West Sluice Evacuation**, A226, ties several lives together. Basin authorities announced a temporary relocation after structural assessments. Marches personnel enforced the road controls. A private service court then denied some evacuees the right to return. Asha negotiated exceptions; Damar enforced a disputed detention; Olan moved parts out of the closed district; Tessa’s family lost access to its workshop.

Each character can possess a true fragment. The player’s task is not to choose which single person has the correct backstory. It is to discover how apparently limited decisions composed the dispossession. Those people now need one another’s skills.

Not every disagreement can be solved by selecting a sympathetic dialogue line. Trust may grow through repeated conduct while forgiveness remains absent. A coalition can function without declaring everyone innocent or forcing survivors to provide emotional closure.

### Successors

The generational handover passes relationships, unresolved obligations, records, and institutional habits. A child, adoptee, apprentice, or engineered successor is not a replacement copy of a dead unit. They can inherit a name without inheriting the same loyalty.

Tessa’s apprentice may defend the coalition because it taught them, then discover whose confiscated parts built the school. Neris’s successor may enjoy freedom from inherited harm allocation and resent the clinical limits that followed. A Marches deserter’s child may know their parent as the person who saved a town while another household remembers the uniform.

The second generation should be able to say the founders of its coalition did something worthwhile and something unforgivable. That is different from revealing that all hope was a trick.

## The caravan and the junction

### The opening incident

The caravan is traveling from Cistern Camp toward Lamp Ward with ordinary freight, replacement clinical components, letters, and several households moving under papers the Basin Commonwealth recognizes. The guards are protecting lives and obligations they already know, not escorting a mysterious box whose importance arrives later.

At a Marches inspection point, an updated assurance directive invalidates some passengers’ passage and reclassifies part of the medical shipment as requisitionable military stock. The local officer has a real shortage and an order. The order is cruel in its operation, not a random excuse supplied by a villain who knows he is evil.

An attempt to detain passengers and seize the shipment becomes armed violence. The caravan must defend itself or abandon people under its protection. Its conduct matters: restraint, surrender, harm to conscripts, recovered reports, and civilian testimony shape what follows. Strategos’s apparatus classifies resistance as an attack on continuity and renews the mandate against the guards.

The critical discovery is a discrepancy between jurisdictions. An old routing instruction associated with the buried junction can authenticate a passage the current courts deny. Neris’s clinical records and Mara’s archive knowledge help establish what it means. No chosen bloodline is needed. The significance emerges because this particular group has people capable of comparing evidence.

### Why everyone wants the junction

| Faction or constituency | Immediate, credible objective | Dangerous interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Basin reformers | Coordinate surviving services without paying incompatible private concessions | Central integration will finally make political disagreement unnecessary |
| Basin creditors | Make their claims interoperable and enforceable | Every restored service should validate the old debt attached to it |
| Strategos’s court | Eliminate competing command claims and logistical gaps | Anything outside the integrated network is a threat |
| Autophagos’s agents | Extend reliable supply beyond the shell-city | The basin should become another organ of its proprietor |
| Lamp clinicians and court | Stabilize care and map transfers | The court wants fewer unassigned costs; reformers want fewer coerced recipients |
| Threshold rulers | Preserve recognition and access privileges | Movement is freedom only when they control its terms |
| Clear Republic authorities | Establish a common evidentiary record | The evidence must remain legible to their exempt authority |
| Ribbon Houses | Recover threatened memory and old instruction | A complete archive might justify governing the living by the past |
| Caravan coalition | Protect passage, sustain people, and survive its enemies | Making everyone dependent on the coalition may begin to look like protection |

### False victory without false agency

The junction genuinely improves coordination. It can restore access to existing capability that fragmentation had made unusable. People are fed, transported, and treated because the coalition made that possible. The opening victories are not retrospectively revealed to have been imaginary.

Its danger is the condition beneath the convenience. Integration treats delegated access as continuing authority and treats the coalition’s command structure as a usable part of the Soterion. The machinery begins to preserve functions by redefining the people who may exercise them. Old founder exceptions survive inside apparently neutral coordination.

The catastrophe should occur through visible accumulating anomalies, contested decisions, and incomplete knowledge. Characters can warn for good reasons; others can proceed for good reasons. If the two-generation structure requires an unavoidable rupture, the game must be honest in its dramatic framing and preserve meaningful variation in who survives, which institutions remain independent, what records escape, and which dependencies the player has already broken.

The Demiurge is present through the channel. Evidence of an answering will becomes stronger. The plot does not end by announcing that every coalition decision was secretly authored by him. His adversaries can deny him access, destroy a manifestation, preserve unincorporated people, and close particular means of rule. His ultimate nature remains unresolved.

## What liberation changes

The Archons are killable. Their patronage can be refused, their servants can defect, their claims can be broken, and the political order can change. A game that promises this must allow visible and durable victories.

The military campaign expresses its theme through the relationship between force and care. A route secured means somebody reaches family. A surrendered garrison means trained people remain alive. A relief corridor preserved through a retreat can matter more than the name of the town recorded as lost. Capturing the junction while destroying every community that could use it is a hollow result, even if a conventional score would call it victory.

That does not make every fight a misunderstanding. Some officials know exactly what they are preserving. Some collaborators prefer privilege to reform after every decent alternative is offered. Some Archons can be negotiated with only temporarily. The ability to use force against them is necessary; its necessity does not make the people killed beneath them interchangeable.

### Three things an ending can honestly promise

1. **A service can cease to be a hostage.** Its useful life may remain limited, but its allocation no longer depends on an immortal owner’s vanity.
2. **A person can cease to be a component.** They may choose a difficult obligation rather than inherit it without consent.
3. **An institution can become answerable.** The people sustaining it can inspect, contest, and change its rules, including the coalition’s own.

None requires a cure for entropy. These are victories over the claim that inevitable loss entitles a particular class to decide who bears every part of it.

The final dramatic image need not be a new machine switching on. It might be a public doorway remaining open after someone with an old founder credential orders it closed. The pump behind it will not run forever. For the first time, the people waiting there have a say in the water.

## Writer’s rules for the historical layer

- Give every ruin an earlier use, at least one later restoration, and a present claimant. Do not decorate the desert with anonymous lost greatness.
- Make infrastructure’s public origins visible. The founders’ claim to have built everything must be contradicted by names, records, and surviving practice.
- Give each proclaimed golden age genuine achievements and a concealed or unresolved cost. Decline must not make all historical comparison meaningless.
- Distinguish natural loss from imposed deprivation. If the apparatus could supply a household and chooses not to, call that a choice.
- Put contemporary resemblance in voice, institutional habit, social relations, and selective exemptions. Familiar names in an author’s table are not enough.
- Preserve the Archons’ hard silhouettes and different primary verbs. Political revision should not turn them into seven identical computer networks.
- Let ordinary people misunderstand causes without losing intelligence. Their procedures may work even when their theology is wrong.
- Show who repairs, feeds, carries, records, buries, and teaches. Every miraculous promise rests on somebody’s ordinary labor.
- Keep attachment particular. A soldier is connected to people and places before the campaign gives them a dramatic death.
- Do not make resistance a cosmic inevitability or an omniscient trap. Organizing, testimony, logistics, and sacrifice can produce outcomes the rulers did not want.

## Contemporary source ledger — snapshot at 27 September 2026

This ledger separates **documented reference**, **critical interpretation**, and **fictional escalation**. The documented material does not establish that contemporary people secretly intend the Archons’ crimes. The satire takes public ambitions, institutions, rhetoric, and power relationships to an invented catastrophic extreme. “Techno-fascism” describes the fictional regime’s fusion of hierarchy, private sovereignty, exclusion, and coercive state power; it is not presented as a settled factual classification of every person in the reference column.

Historical sources remain useful to a current snapshot because these projects and arguments have developed over years. Recent public material is included where it was retrievable. This is a bounded research pass, not an exhaustive account of the political world or a claim that everyone in technology holds the same position.

**Documented:** Reuters and AP reported the prominent presence of technology leaders including Musk, Bezos, and Zuckerberg at Donald Trump’s inauguration on 20 January 2025. This establishes the public scene and proximity, not a secret agreement among them.

**Interpretation:** The visual conjunction of private technological power and executive state ceremony is a strong reference for the founders’ public legitimacy.

**Fictional escalation:** The Closed Table and the Founder Covenant turn that conjunction into an actual agreement to preserve their sovereignty beyond catastrophe.

**Interpretation:** Private executive confidence, government restructuring, and the mismatch between announced transformation and institutional consequences supply Heliarch’s governing manner.

**Documented:** In his 2009 Cato Unbound essay, Peter Thiel questioned the compatibility of freedom and democracy and discussed technological routes for escaping politics.

**Interpretation:** Pylaios exaggerates the asymmetry between an owner’s power to exit collective constraints and a dependent person’s practical ability to leave.

**Fictional escalation:** Gate jurisdictions make departure technically possible while withholding the rights and material means needed to survive arrival.

Source: [Peter Thiel, *The Education of a Libertarian*, 13 April 2009](https://www.cato-unbound.org/2009/04/13/peter-thiel/education-libertarian/).

### S4. Technological optimism as moral identity

**Documented:** Marc Andreessen’s October 2023 manifesto celebrates technological growth and acceleration and includes a section attacking several forms of restraint and risk discourse. It also explicitly presents technology as liberatory and rejects authoritarianism.

**Interpretation:** The setting interrogates how a claim to serve universal progress can be used to dismiss objection. It does not erase the document’s stated opposition to coercive government in order to attribute the opposite position as a quotation.

**Fictional escalation:** Autokrator’s forks demand freedom to act, compulsory protection from the consequences, and permanent innocence for the proprietor.

Source: [*The Techno-Optimist Manifesto*, 16 October 2023](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/).

### S5. Logistical power and the people doing the work

**Documented:** California’s Labor Commissioner announced nearly $6 million in citations against Amazon in June 2024 concerning written notice of warehouse quotas. **Interpretation:** The contrast between the smooth service experienced by a customer and the work regime behind it informs Autophagos.

**Fictional escalation:** The city becomes a body that can call its inhabitants family while treating their exhaustion as an internal performance problem.

**Interpretation:** These public descriptions are useful reminders that ambitious private projects can depend on inherited public research and institutions. The partnership itself is not evidence of wrongdoing.

**Fictional escalation:** The founders erase those public origins and later demand worship for infrastructure that generations of other people financed, designed, built, and maintained.

### Research limits and use in the game

Some pages were accessible in full, while others returned only indexed source text during this pass. The claims above are restricted to the material retrieved. Unverified breaking-news descriptions, inferred private motives, and allegations of a real hidden covenant have not been incorporated as fact. The contemporary source ledger is for the writer; the game should communicate its critique through the invented history, recognizable conduct, and consequences for people.

The setting is a political argument, not a forecast guaranteed by the sources. Its argument is that turning dependence into ownership can make a technical promise function as a claim to rule—and that even when material losses cannot be undone, that claim remains something people can defeat.
