# Achlydesa — Campaign and Character Bible

**Draft 1 · 28 September 2026**  
**Companion to the World Bible, Field Atlas Style and UI Bible, and Autonomous Squad Technical Design.**

This document turns the established history into a playable political campaign. The user's three decisions are fixed: the caravan's defenders become an insurgent army through their victories; the general remains invisible as an individual; and an optional encounter inside the Soterion permits a fight against the Demiurge through a proxy, a partial historical revelation, and an expulsion followed by an unexplained warning. New names, scenes, and campaign arrangements below are proposed story material.

The campaign opens in A238. It retains the existing two-generation structure. The prose serial's drifter is a separate protagonist and has no required role here.

## 1. The story in one page

A caravan carrying replacement medical components, ordinary freight, letters, and relocating families is stopped under a newly revised security directive. Its passage was legal when it departed. Now some of its passengers are classified as unauthorized dependents, and its medical cargo belongs to an army that never purchased it.

The defenders resist. They survive because an unseen commander coordinates a withdrawal that the checkpoint's doctrine does not anticipate. Their first military achievement is getting people away alive.

The caravan cannot simply reach another town and dissolve. Strategos's assurance network extends its pursuit to anyone who shelters the fugitives. A town gives them refuge; the defenders must help it survive the consequences. They reopen a relief road, accept a garrison's surrender, and defeat an attempt to isolate several settlements. Other escorts, deserters, municipal soldiers, and excluded households gather around them. Victory creates the obligations that turn a caravan guard into an insurgent army.

The title of general arrives after these achievements. It recognizes a command that already exists. Different communities recognize it for different reasons, and recognition never amounts to ownership of those communities.

The coalition discovers that a buried Soterion junction can recognize rights and coordinate services which the archons' courts deliberately keep incompatible. It could make the liberated corridor viable. It also contains the machinery by which the old founders made their authority permanent.

Taking the junction therefore becomes a political struggle before it becomes a siege. The coalition needs allies whose goals conflict: public provision, municipal independence, medical survival, debt enforcement, private escape, historical restitution. Several genuinely useful bargains would preserve the architecture of domination.

The first generation can win territory, kill an archon, and restore real freedoms. It then confronts the Joining: an attempt by the surviving Soterion network to convert administrative coordination into permanent jurisdiction over people. The player determines how far that attempt succeeds locally, what survives, and which promises become institutions. Roughly twenty years later, those institutions and their failures are the second generation's inheritance.

The final struggle concerns whether the surviving basin can live without a proprietor. The optional descent into the Soterion reveals an agency behind the founder covenant and permits the army to sever its local means of acting. Expelling it does not repair the universe. It does establish that neither ancient ownership nor incomprehensible power makes obedience inevitable.

## 2. The unseen general

The player is present as command authority, not as an authored individual. No name entry, portrait, body, prescribed childhood, voiced dialogue, combat statistics, romance, bloodline, or secret identity is necessary. Characters address the player as Commander and, later, General. They remember the player's decisions rather than describing an invented personality.

At the opening, the player already coordinates the caravan's defense. Ione Var is its visible escort captain: she translates intent into field action, disputes bad plans, and has relationships with the people being protected. Neither a special birthright nor a newly invented prelude is needed to explain the player's competence. The campaign establishes competence through play.

Player choices take the form of orders, negotiated commitments, appointments, and responses to petitions. A policy card might say **Authorize safe passage for surrendered personnel**, not supply a paragraph of first-person dialogue. Characters can interpret those choices differently.

The general's presence does not confer universal awareness. Orders originate from an actual headquarters, and reports arrive through the established communications simulation. Named officers can command forward headquarters; switching command scope follows the existing UI rules. The invisible player role does not let a distant general speak directly into an isolated squad's mind.

The command office persists through the generational transition. The story does not establish whether the unseen person is the same, has a successor, or how old they are. It never uses that absence to hint at immortality. New characters inherit orders and institutions, not a revelation about the avatar. Named chiefs of staff and field commanders age, retire, die, and succeed one another normally.

The player is accountable through material consequences. Settlements can withdraw support, officers can refuse unlawful commitments, and a military council can dispute authority. These are conflicts with a command, not attempts to psychoanalyze a silent protagonist.

## 3. How the defenders become an army

### Three victories and a recognition

These are a dramatic sequence, not three mandatory kill counts. Their objectives can be achieved by maneuver, relief, negotiation backed by force, or combat.

| Stage | What the defenders accomplish | Why more people join | What the commander now owes |
|---|---|---|---|
| The caravan withdrawal | Save a viable body of travelers and escape the checkpoint's pursuit | Survivors remain together because dispersal exposes them to detention | Food, medical care, a destination, and an honest account of casualties |
| The open road | Break the isolation of Reedbank, the town that sheltered them | Local watch units and other caravan escorts see a force capable of keeping promises | Continued access rather than one ceremonial delivery |
| The surrendered garrison | Obtain personnel, stores, and a defensible crossing without treating every conscript as an enemy | Defectors can join a command that distinguishes surrender from treachery | Enforceable surrender terms and protection from collective reprisals |
| The Three Crossings campaign | Prevent a converging Marches operation from cutting the corridor into isolated towns | Municipal companies and organized workers commit to common operations | A strategy for the region and limits on emergency military authority |

After Three Crossings, delegates and unit representatives recognize the existing commander as **General of the Free Passage Army**. The name begins as a description of what the force protects. Its later inadequacy becomes a political issue: does free passage include a right to settle, receive treatment, refuse conscription, or leave the coalition?

The recognition scene is understated. There is no throne or coronation. Asha brings a document with incompatible signatures and an empty space where the term of command should be. Ione explains that companies already follow the general's orders. Asha replies that this makes the unanswered question more urgent.

The **Passage Articles** provide a starting settlement: protection of noncombatants; recorded requisitions with avenues for restitution; surrender terms binding on subordinate commands; civilian representation; and a defined process for ending emergency authority. The player can accept, narrow, or postpone specific provisions. Refusal has visible political costs but does not instantly dissolve the army.

### Recruitment is a bargain with a person

The roster grows because operations expose missing capabilities. A bridge cannot be held without engineers; a surrendered company needs an officer who can integrate it; a clinic's protection requires knowing which households absorb its injuries.

Every important recruitment specifies five things: the immediate need, what the recruit wants, what they bring besides fighting ability, who objects, and the promise that remains after recruitment. Joining does not erase other loyalties. A municipal company may serve only while its home remains protected. An officer can leave command while continuing to support the cause.

Avoid a repetitive structure in which every recruit requires a private rescue mission. People also join through negotiated surrender, shared work, organizational affiliation, testimony, a protected withdrawal, or the fulfillment of a public commitment.

## 4. The opening: the Red Ledger incident

**Route:** Cistern Camp to Lamp Ward, via the Dry Meridian road. Reedbank lies on a secondary river crossing. The first pursuit and relief operations occupy several days; stopping to treat the wounded changes the pursuers' position. Distances belong to the world map, not a sequence of encounter rooms.

The player initially has Ione, Tessa, Petra, Eren, several unnamed escort squads, and ordinary transport personnel. Neris travels with the medical freight. Mara is a passenger carrying mundane testimony alongside older records. Neither is introduced as the bearer of a magical key.

At the inspection station, **Captain Varo Kest** receives a directive whose effective date precedes the caravan's departure. He has wounded soldiers, inadequate clinical supplies, and promotion prospects tied to recovering requisitioned stock. He chooses to enforce the order despite knowing that the civilian papers were valid. This is a decision, not a misunderstanding which one kind conversation solves.

The player can seek time, move vulnerable passengers out of reach, prepare a covered withdrawal, or attempt a negotiated exchange. Negotiation can release people and improve positioning. Kest's standing orders still require impoundment of the disputed travelers; an official extraction team advances if the guards do not surrender them. The player decides how to resist. The story never requires the caravan to fire on people merely to start the plot.

The tactical objective is passage and survival. Seizing the station, destroying every defender, or capturing Kest is optional. An orderly retreat with lost cargo can be a success. Surrendering some cargo may save lives while creating a clinical shortage later. Casualties and promises are carried forward without a hidden perfect-outcome requirement.

Afterward, an old roadside terminal recognizes one of the rejected travel claims under a dormant public-service routing instruction. This is the first sign that the current courts' authority is neither universal nor technically necessary. Neris notices the same exception in medical transfer records; Mara recognizes its wording from the Basin Compact's inherited service agreements.

Strategos's court then expands the enforcement mandate. The caravan's documented survival is described as an attack on continuity. The absurd language has real effects: Reedbank's relief contracts are suspended because it provided water to the fugitives.

**The inciting commitment:** the defenders could abandon Reedbank and remain itinerant fugitives. Their decision to reopen its relief road turns self-defense into insurgency. Frame this openly as the campaign's premise. Permit several plans and negotiated degrees of support, rather than presenting departure as an equally supported campaign which the game cannot actually deliver.

## 5. Campaign architecture

The campaign has fixed dramatic questions and flexible operations. A situation opens on the existing map, with forces and deadlines derived from the simulation. It does not transport the army to a separate tactical arena. Failure changes the next situation wherever possible.

| Movement | Main question | Operational center | Political consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. People under protection | Whom does this command refuse to abandon? | Caravan withdrawal; Reedbank relief | The first obligations outlive the contract |
| II. A name for the army | Who can authorize common force? | Garrison surrender; Three Crossings | Recognition as general; Passage Articles |
| III. The price of allies | Which dependencies can be broken, and which bargains are tolerable? | Regional operations around clinics, gates, relays, and the shell-city | Coalition capable of contesting the junction |
| IV. The first settlement | What does liberation entitle the army to govern? | Campaign against Strategos; capture or denial of the junction | Real improvements; the Joining and its containment |
| V. The inheritance | Who owns a revolution after its founders? | Renewed fronts roughly twenty years later | Successors contest the coalition's institutions |
| VI. No proprietor | Can shared services exist without permanent personal sovereignty? | Return to the junction; optional interior operation | A regional settlement and consequences that persist |

### Movement I — People under protection

Reedbank's rescue gives the caravan a provisional home. Sera can open a less exposed approach, but it passes through an excluded settlement whose residents distrust anyone carrying official papers. The player can negotiate shared use, improve its defenses, or impose passage at the cost of future support. Sera's willingness to join rests on conduct here, not a flattering reply.

Olan offers access to a supposedly empty reserve depot. There are useful stocks, but fewer than its certified returns promise. Recovering them wins time. Opening the books reveals that the Commonwealth's ability to support the region is partly fictional. The commander must decide how to disclose the shortage while preventing a rush on the depot.

Eren's first serious scene concerns a captured conscript who attended the same travel-paper examinations. He wants the prisoner treated fairly and also wants the veterans to see him as competent. The prisoner need not become a recruit. An ordinary life allowed to continue is a sufficient consequence.

### Movement II — A name for the army

Damar is encountered advising a Marches garrison whose supply guarantees have failed. He can arrange surrender and bring disciplined personnel into the coalition. Tessa identifies him as the officer who ordered her brother's detention during the West Sluice Evacuation.

Recruitment is not a binary choice between endorsing his crimes and killing useful soldiers. Possible terms include testimony, removal from police authority, a time-limited operational appointment, preservation of evidence, and a later civilian hearing. Rejecting Damar does not cancel surrender protections for his soldiers. The garrison's engineers and clerks provide slower alternative routes to his operational knowledge.

Three Crossings tests whether the growing force can coordinate dispersed defenses, relief transport, reconnaissance, and reserves. The enemy need not be annihilated. Keeping two communities connected and forcing the offensive to culminate can secure recognition even if a third crossing is lost. The political settlement acknowledges the people whose homes paid for that success.

The recognition of the general closes this movement. From here onward, military success also creates scrutiny. An army capable of protecting rights is capable of violating them at regional scale.

### Movement III — The price of allies

The player chooses the order and extent of four regional arcs. The campaign requires a viable combination of transport, clinical support, communications, and political access. It does not require every named character or every archon to be defeated.

**Lamp Ward: the patients outside the count.** Neris and the clinic workers can document how Anodyne's court exports injuries. Public disclosure helps victims organize but can prompt the court to terminate services on which identifiable people depend. Evacuation capacity, ordinary treatment, voluntary assistance, and military protection can reduce the harm; no dialogue option invents a replacement medical system. Anodyne can be fought, isolated from particular recipient networks, or forced into a temporary concession. Neris's parent has their own view of the risks and is not simply a hostage who approves every choice.

**The western gate: the price of arrival.** Korin offers passage through a Threshold concession if the coalition validates the principal's arrival records. Those records recognize some people and erase others. The physical destruction inherent in gate transit makes the court's claims about identity disturbing without requiring the narrative to settle personal continuity. A slower surface corridor can defeat the concession's monopoly. It consumes scarce time and transport but prevents a political bargain from becoming mandatory.

**The common relay: who may remain private.** Leont can help seize a Clear Republic relay and expose the surveillance permissions used to prepare reprisals. Aletheia offers evidence against Strategos while demanding continuing access to coalition communications. Publishing everything would also expose excluded households, informants, and clinic recipients. Establishing a protected evidentiary process is work performed by named civilian partners, not an automatic compromise button. Aletheia treats limits on her access as censorship while regarding her own sealed chamber as a basic dignity.

**The moving district: a city inside its owner.** Petra identifies a window in which relatives and willing residents can leave Autophagos. Some residents refuse evacuation because the city still feeds them reliably. Others seek collective bargaining instead of an externally imposed rescue. The coalition can support an exit corridor, organize a supply refusal, or bargain for passage. Assaulting Autophagos puts a populated body at risk. There is no concealed switch that cleanly separates every resident from the monster; the player receives an honest assessment before committing.

Heliarch's movements alter the wider theater. An announced tour of the sky becomes a weather and fire threat, with delayed oil ignition changing route safety. His court is furious about inadequate celebration while officers are trying to predict which wells will burn. Mneme's crossings and archives supply routes and evidence with conflicting ownership claims. These archons need not each occupy a mandatory boss chapter.

### Movement IV — The first settlement

Strategos is the principal first-generation antagonist because the army's existence contradicts his right to determine which organized force is legitimate. His servants offer local ceasefires that would permanently classify the coalition as an auxiliary. Accepting a temporary armistice can be useful; accepting permanent command integration is a consequential political commitment.

The decisive campaign has three connected objectives: split the assurance network's logistical support, make surrender credible to its personnel, and neutralize the weapons sheltering Strategos's brain. His fear produces lethal escalation but does not absolve him. He helped build the system that treats his personal security as everyone's obligation.

The player can kill Strategos or achieve a constrained regional victory by breaking his command network and denying him the junction. Killing him is permanent. His weapons may still need to be disarmed; his legal office may continue issuing inherited instructions until its infrastructure is addressed. A surviving Strategos remains a known external threat, not an excuse to erase the victory.

The captured junction performs useful work immediately when limited functions are restored: routing existing supplies, recognizing rejected claims, and exposing discrepancies between service courts. People experience this as liberation. The temptation to expand its authority is credible because the first benefits are real.

### The Joining — a crisis with different outcomes

The junction is one surviving node of a larger Soterion network. Other courts are already restoring links to it. The first-generation finale concerns a regional synchronization attempt, not a curse automatically triggered by touching the correct artifact.

Evidence arrives before commitment: maintenance workers receive appointments they did not accept; a dead administrator's credential outranks a living council; emergency supply authorization is interpreted as permanent jurisdiction over recipients. Tessa can demonstrate one dependency physically. Korin or surviving clerks can demonstrate another in the records. No single missing recruit hides every warning.

The player can limit delegation, maintain independent dispatch and power, evacuate endangered districts, isolate approaches, or permit deeper integration in exchange for urgent services. Rival factions also act. Their ability to force reconnection depends on infrastructure they actually hold; it is not a scripted teleport behind the player's defenses.

| First-settlement result | What happens | What the second generation inherits |
|---|---|---|
| Broad integration | The Joining incorporates much of the coalition's service and command administration; some people are excluded or trapped by reassigned authority | A powerful continuity regime, preserved services with coercive conditions, and surviving resistance enclaves |
| Emergency severance | The army stops incorporation after it begins, accepting a difficult withdrawal and loss of linked services | Independent towns, avoidable and unavoidable losses to investigate, and a sealed junction that still has rival claimants |
| Prepared containment | Earlier isolation, dispersed authority, and successful operations prevent local mass incorporation | A real first-generation success; a weaker but organized federation facing renewed reconnection attempts and shrinking material capacity |

Prepared containment must be possible. It is expensive and requires foregoing some short-term benefits, but the game must not manufacture an unstoppable betrayal to punish competent play. The time jump remains in every branch; a compulsory twenty-year passage does not require an identical compulsory massacre. Wider Soterion disturbances and continuing decay explain a changed world without taking away the region the player actually saved.

### Movement V — The inheritance

The handover occurs roughly twenty years after the first settlement, using its actual campaign date. The opening record separates confirmed institutional history, contested testimony, and current reports. Old atlas information is visibly dated. The player inherits strategic knowledge, not accurate live positions from twenty years ago.

The new army's immediate purpose depends on the settlement. It may liberate communities from a continuity regime, reunite a fractured corridor, or defend a functioning federation from forced reconnection. This is more than changing the introductory narration: garrisons, public access, supply relationships, legal claims, and recruitment conditions differ.

The first operation concerns an ordinary service at the edge of failure: a relief convoy cannot reach a clinic because a checkpoint invokes a founding emergency clause. The insignia above the checkpoint may be the coalition's own. A successor asks why the law still says temporary.

The player cannot solve twenty years of politics with an apology from an unseen avatar. The army must change present conduct and negotiate authority with people who have lived under the inherited settlement.

### Movement VI — No proprietor

The final campaign returns to the junction because the surviving service system remains vulnerable to founder jurisdiction. The public objective is to end that jurisdiction in the basin while preserving as much usable capacity as possible. Destroying every linked machine is one possible denial strategy, with severe consequences; it is not the uniquely virtuous answer.

The climax combines external defense, civilian withdrawal, work on inherited control systems, and a contested transfer of authority. Individual battles occur on the same world grid and clock. Some allies may defend the junction while opposing the commander's preferred government afterward.

The optional interior operation can occur during this campaign or its secured aftermath if the player has maintained access. It resolves a local supernatural threat, not the political negotiations. Killing a proxy cannot decide who owns a cistern or who answers for an unlawful detention.

## 6. First-generation characters

Ages below are at A238. These twelve characters form the central relationship cast, not the total army roster. Field officers, specialists, civilian partners, and archival witnesses have different roles. The army includes ordinary squads whose members matter without each requiring a personal chapter.

### Ione Var, 41 — escort captain; potential chief of staff

**Entrance and purpose:** present from the opening. She handles the visible field command while the player coordinates the whole defense. Her former Basin service provides competence and contacts, not an automatic claim to senior rank over everyone who joins.

**Desire:** get her people through one more passage without accepting another dishonest contract. Her danger is narrowing the meaning of “her people” when the coalition grows.

**Conflict:** she trusts Damar's military judgment and knows what his signature enabled. Protecting him from scrutiny would repeat the institutional loyalty she claims to have left behind.

**Arc:** Ione can become a chief of staff who insists on clear limits and prepares others to replace her, or the indispensable veteran whose personal circle dominates appointments. She argues about implementation and obligations; she does not speak the player's inner thoughts. She keeps bad caravan songs alive and objects when anyone improves their lyrics.

**Death or absence:** another officer can fill the staff function. The army loses her relationships and particular authority; later scenes do not put her exact words in a substitute's mouth.

### Tessa Ruun, 35 — recovery and maintenance chief

**Entrance and purpose:** present from the opening, keeping the oldest carrier moving. Her crews recover, repair, and teach inherited practice. They never unlock a technology tree.

**Desire:** establish a workshop where apprentices can remain instead of being dispersed by eviction, requisition, or the next emergency. She enjoys precise work and dreadful practical jokes involving harmless mislabeling.

**Conflict:** Damar ordered the detention that killed her brother at West Sluice. Olan knows why parts from her family's closed workshop appeared elsewhere. Their usefulness does not make her objections inconvenient misunderstandings.

**Arc:** she can help build public maintenance capacity, leave military service to run a civilian school, or lose faith in a coalition that repeatedly treats skilled labor as requisitionable property. She need never forgive Damar. Cooperation under enforceable limits is a complete relationship outcome.

**Recruitment obligation:** starting loyalty is to the caravan and her crew, not indefinite military mobilization. Keeping her requires honoring workshop and apprenticeship commitments in practice.

### Neris Vale, 29 — orderly; organizer of clinical relief

**Entrance and purpose:** a medical passenger who becomes an ally through the caravan crisis. Continued service requires protection for patients whose records identify them as recipients of transferred harm.

**Desire:** make the excluded visible as patients without turning their histories into public spectacle. Neris's parent, **Halen Vale**, still depends on Anodyne's care and can disagree with the proposed liberation.

**Conflict:** Leont reported Neris's mentor for breaking a real rule. That rule concealed a larger abuse, but his action cannot be rewritten as knowingly malicious to make reconciliation easy.

**Arc:** Neris can create an independent clinical association that bargains with the army, remain trapped in personal exceptions for particular patients, or leave after the command knowingly exports harm. They remember birthdays with embarrassing accuracy and are impatient with inspirational speeches delivered in treatment rooms.

**Limits:** Neris can explain observed transfers and organize care, not divine the full machinery or promise equivalent treatment after severance.

### Olan Vey, 62 — quartermaster with compromised books

**Entrance and purpose:** recruited through the reserve-depot operation. He brings inventories, transport relationships, and a habit of noticing which confident promise is physically impossible.

**Desire:** prevent panic and starvation. He has repeatedly told himself that truthful figures can wait until the next delivery.

**Conflict:** he signed false reserve returns and moved parts from West Sluice. Some decisions kept other communities alive; concealing them also protected officials and denied the dispossessed any remedy. Both facts remain true.

**Arc:** he can give evidence and build accountable allocation practices, retain informal control by claiming nobody else understands the books, or be removed while cooperating as a witness. His hearing concerns specific decisions and victims rather than a single all-purpose morality score. He cooks elaborately with monotonous ingredients and treats sharing a recipe as a serious promise.

**Succession:** training civilian and military replacements is an early need. After the time jump he may be retired, frail, or dead; the story must not require an eighty-plus-year-old quartermaster to remain on campaign.

### Sera Pell, 24 — reconnaissance leader

**Entrance and purpose:** encountered during Reedbank relief. She joins when the army demonstrates that excluded communities can refuse a demand without becoming targets.

**Desire:** make the minor routes usable by residents without making residents legible to every court. She can dislike official recognition even when allies regard it as a gift.

**Conflict:** her younger cousin **Jori Pell, 21**, serves willingly in a Marches garrison. Jori considers her work profitable evasion of obligations which poorer conscripts still fulfill.

**Arc:** Sera can organize a respected reconnaissance service, become an unaccountable keeper of secret routes, or return to civilian guiding. Jori can surrender, remain an opponent, or cooperate without joining. Family access improves the opportunity for contact, never guarantees defection. Sera collects regional words for weather and corrects maps that erase inhabited places.

### Damar Senn, 52 — former Marches officer

**Entrance and purpose:** a conditional recruit after the garrison surrender. He understands assurance operations, unit discipline, and the practical weaknesses of Strategos's forces.

**Desire:** serve a command whose competence he can respect. At first he mistakes this for having resolved the moral question of service.

**Conflict:** his responsibility for Tessa's brother's death is established. The investigation may reveal other participants, but never a secret document proving him innocent.

**Arc:** the strongest outcome is truthful testimony, acceptance of restricted authority and judgment, and useful service without a claim to forgiveness. An alternative is a respected operational specialist who refuses full accountability, dividing the coalition. Repeated protection from scrutiny can make him an advocate for the army's own permanent emergency powers. He repairs bindings on cheap books and hates being thanked for basic professionalism.

**Limits:** he cannot personally decrypt every enemy order or replace reconnaissance. Declining his recruitment must leave viable, costlier alternatives.

### Leont Aras, 38 — communications specialist

**Entrance and purpose:** joins during the common-relay arc after the coalition protects vulnerable sources rather than publishing their identities indiscriminately.

**Desire:** preserve the original promise of public scrutiny while admitting that his family's status came from controlling what scrutiny could reach.

**Conflict:** he correctly identified Neris's mentor's rule violation and trusted the institution to interpret it fairly. His temptation is to repair the institution by accumulating better surveillance.

**Arc:** Leont can establish communications with limited, inspectable authority, or supply the technical competence for a new security apparatus. Apologizing to Neris opens a conversation; relinquishing privileged access supplies evidence of change. He is a committed amateur performer with excellent timing and an unfortunate need to explain jokes afterward.

**Military role:** relay planning, interception assessment, and keeping separated headquarters in contact. He cannot remove transmission delays or know unobserved enemy intentions.

### Mara Den, 78 — traveling archive keeper

**Entrance and purpose:** present as a civilian passenger. She shares testimony through a partnership; recruitment never means putting the elderly archivist in a combat squad.

**Desire:** keep records available for people who must act on them, not merely preserve objects behind another hereditary gate. She remembers the Second Restoration's actual improvements and its evasions.

**Conflict:** her evidence implicates useful allies and undermines flattering stories of resistance. She also knows that indiscriminate disclosure can endanger living witnesses.

**Arc:** the player helps distribute copies and custodians, accepts restrictive ownership in exchange for access, or loses an archive through neglect or military appropriation. Mara likes gossip and insists it is a separate professional category from testimony. She refuses to turn every ordinary memory into a lesson.

**Succession:** she is expected to die or retire before the second generation. Her preserved collection, its omissions, and her students matter more than prolonging her life to deliver exposition.

### Petra Oss, 32 — transport leader

**Entrance and purpose:** present from the opening. A former Shell route driver, she knows how schedules, load limits, and household obligations intersect.

**Desire:** give her relatives an actual choice about leaving Autophagos. Some want to remain. She cannot speak for every resident because she once drove their route.

**Conflict:** the coalition needs transport for operations while an evacuation window is closing. This should be a scheduling and commitment problem with multiple preparations, not a surprise ultimatum that makes one life arbitrarily cost a whole army.

**Arc:** Petra may organize a civilian transport cooperative, accept a senior military role, or leave to maintain family routes. She dislikes being praised for endurance and is fiercely opinionated about food. If the army attacks the shell-city without addressing residents' survival, she can refuse participation while still protecting other coalition civilians.

### Eren Tal, 19 — guard trainee; potential junior officer

**Entrance and purpose:** present from the opening. He sought wages, recognized travel, and an apprenticeship, not a historic role.

**Desire:** be taken seriously without becoming someone he would have feared before joining. He practices signatures because he imagines authority arriving as the ability to sign things.

**Conflict:** veteran cynicism attracts him until he sees it used to dismiss avoidable suffering. Praise for aggressive action can encourage him to confuse decisiveness with leadership.

**Arc:** Eren can become a capable officer, choose the civilian apprenticeship he originally wanted, grow into an enforcer of emergency authority, or die before any of these futures. He has no concealed inheritance or guaranteed promotion. If alive after the jump, he is a middle-aged witness to how the army's habits formed, not an eternally youthful mascot.

### Asha Ren, 44 — Reedbank's municipal negotiator

**Entrance and purpose:** a civilian ally from the first relief operation. She remains answerable to Reedbank; she is not automatically absorbed into the headquarters staff.

**Desire:** make old public rights enforceable without making her town a military dependency. Her West Sluice negotiations saved particular families and left others outside the exceptions.

**Conflict:** emergency measures she once accepted as temporary reappear as efficient proposals from her own allies. She can also privilege Reedbank's needs over neighboring communities while using the language of universal rights.

**Arc:** Asha helps establish a civilian settlement, bargains for local immunity at others' expense, or leads political opposition to an army she still wants to survive. She keeps a balcony garden of ordinary cooking plants and regards speeches about sacrifice as requests for someone else's labor.

**Interaction:** agreements, petitions, and occasional council scenes. The player gives no orders to her town simply because her portrait appears beside friendly units.

### Korin Dey, 27 — arrival clerk; diplomatic liaison

**Entrance and purpose:** encountered at the western gate. Korin can leave the concession with evidence and expertise if protected witnesses receive passage too.

**Desire:** stop deciding which people qualify as continuous with the persons named in their papers. At the same time, being the clerk who can secure an exception is the only power Korin has known.

**Conflict:** saving individuals by manipulating records preserves an institution that requires manipulation. Abolishing the concession's authority may also invalidate claims on which vulnerable arrivals rely.

**Arc:** Korin can transfer discretion into a public appeal process, retain a personal patronage network, or return to a court promising humane exceptions. They love games of elaborate etiquette and are disconcerted by people who say exactly what they want.

**Military role:** negotiation and interpretation of access claims, supported by ordinary clerks. Korin is neither a hacker who opens everything nor an irreplaceable key to the final act.

None of these offices controls every member of its society. Lamp workers can oppose Oris; creditors disagree about solvency; Marches officers can honor a surrender their superiors reject. Political complexity comes from overlapping obligations and power, not from making every apparent ally a concealed villain.

## 8. Relationships that sustain the campaign

| Relationship | Early friction | Playable development | A satisfying unresolved state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ione–Asha | Protection requires discretion; civic authority requires limits | Joint planning for a town the army cannot hold indefinitely | Respect maintained through binding disagreement |

Relationships change through shared operations, evidence, fulfilled promises, and the authority the player grants. Avoid gift grinding, mandatory pairings, and hidden affection thresholds for political decisions. Optional personal scenes provide intimacy; the player does not need to micromanage friendships to keep the army functional.

## 9. The second generation

Successors inherit circumstances, not copied abilities or predetermined opinions. The roster combines former children, apprentices, later arrivals, and surviving first-generation figures. Parenthood is optional; no romance pairing is required to produce the next army.

Dates below give starting ages at A238. Second-generation ages are those ages plus the actual first-campaign duration and the approximately twenty-year interval. For an illustrative two-year first campaign, a child aged eight at the opening is about thirty at the handover, not twenty-eight.

| **Ada Ruun, 13** | Tessa's niece and workshop pupil; a civilian, never a child combat unit | Recovery officer or civilian maintenance representative; joins to protect the schools and workshops sustaining the region | Learns which confiscated parts founded the workshop. Wants restitution that does not close it |

Surviving older characters may return as advisers, civilians, opponents, or field officers where plausible. Their role follows their earlier decisions. Eren is particularly useful as a still-active veteran; Ione may serve in a limited staff role; Tessa may insist that her workshop is no longer a military asset. Mara's students and records normally carry her legacy.

## 10. The optional encounter inside the Soterion

### Purpose and access

The army can finish the regional political campaign without discovering this route. The optional operation offers historical understanding and the removal of a local hostile presence. It does not gate every hopeful ending behind secret completion criteria.

“Inside” means descending through the buried junction into surviving service galleries, control chambers, and anomalous spaces attached to them. These remain terrain on the shared grid, with height, access, communications, and elapsed time. Interior layers connect through known entrances and shafts. There is no separate timeless boss arena while the outside army freezes.

Three practical conditions make the attempt possible:

1. **Evidence of an answering agency.** At least two independent traces establish that behavior exceeds a routine inherited instruction: for example, protected founder recordings contradict later replies, or an isolated chamber responds using information unavailable to its archived process.
2. **A defensible isolation plan.** Crews can cut or hold local authority conduits while sustaining the operation through existing independent power, communications, and a withdrawal route. This uses repaired equipment and known physical procedures, not a new anti-god invention.
3. **An informed political mandate.** People whose services are endangered receive a concrete proposal and representation in the decision. An authoritarian command can proceed without consent, but that creates a real conflict; the presence does not enforce a moral purity lock.

Each condition has several ways to meet it. Archives, captured court staff, surviving service personnel, and direct observation can supply evidence. Several engineers can prepare isolation. No particular recruit, romance, archon kill sequence, or uniquely missable document is required. If the player has destroyed all accessible entrances or the equipment necessary to sustain descent, this route can be unavailable for a clearly stated physical reason.

### What appears

The presence uses a surviving interface called **the Receiver**. This name belongs to the old installation, not to the entity.

Its proxy is an unfinished, seated human figure whose surfaces resemble worn municipal enamel. Its posture remains patient even when the chamber around it changes scale. There is an empty recess where its face ought to be; voices arrive from ordinary inspection speakers at different distances. When it turns, the seated body remains still and its shadow changes seats.

The field atlas represents it with a small, unsettling archival plate and a legible operational footprint. Surreal presentation does not require an expensive animated titan. The art communicates a failure of familiar space; the tactical overlays still communicate reachable terrain, known hazards, and observed targets honestly.

Nothing proves that the figure resembles its occupant. The characters may call the agency the Demiurge after comparing old covenant language, but neither its chosen name nor its nature receives authoritative confirmation. Never subtitle it as “the true god” or provide a complete codex biography.

The Receiver is the smallest visible thing through which the largest reaches: a person-sized interface to a vast, frightened power, which is itself inside something none of the characters can perceive (World Bible §Cosmology). Nothing in the encounter reveals the Being.

*Open design question:* where the campaign's flinches fall (World Bible §The flinch). Proposed: one in Movement I, one at the Joining, one inside the twenty-year gap (visible only in the dated atlas), and one during the Receiver operation.

### The fight as a command operation

The proxy has finite local means of acting: inherited defenses, connected machinery, authority channels, and a physically vulnerable interface. Its wider capabilities remain uncertain. It can use what is connected, but it cannot simply invent unlimited soldiers, replace destroyed equipment, or take control of any loyal person because the script demands it.

| Phase | Pressure | Player orders | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | The network claims authority over access and attempts to close the return route | Hold entrances, protect work parties, establish reserves and independent relay coverage | The army earns a defensible foothold; retreat remains possible |
| Recognition | Linked systems begin accepting the proxy's priorities over local dispatch | Isolate compromised relays, shift units to agreed fallback orders, evacuate exposed workers | Connected advantages diminish as hostile access is removed |
| Exposure | The Receiver concentrates remaining defenses around its interface and threatens an isolation crew | Suppress defenses, rotate exhausted squads, coordinate engineering with the assault | Its local protections fail in an observable sequence |
| Expulsion | A short-lived opportunity permits destruction of the interface while the exit is under pressure | Authorize the assault, sustain containment, recover casualties and withdraw | The proxy is destroyed and the answering presence is driven from this local channel |

The player commands positions, priorities, timing, support, and contingencies. Subordinates execute. No special phase asks the player to click each soldier through a maze or enter an unrelated rhythm minigame.

Agency interference must follow established rules. A false directive enters a compromised communications path; units respond according to authentication and standing orders. The player receives evidence and can sever that path. The game never silently changes an issued order, falsifies the player's own choice history, or makes the interface unusable as a horror effect.

Physical anomalies may alter a marked region after a warning interval. Their tactical result is predictable enough to plan around even when their cause is inexplicable. A corridor's apparent length may disagree with its measured transit time; actual movement and arrival estimates continue to use the simulation's consistent underlying geometry.

### What the encounter reveals

The evidence is recovered during preparation, pauses between assaults, and the secured aftermath. Avoid a speech that stops an ongoing battle to explain the setting.

**Established through records and corroboration:** the founders inherited public achievements they later claimed to have created; selective continuity was a deliberate design requirement; ordinary people were assigned exposure so protected identities could persist; later restorations repeatedly recovered less capacity than the previous one; and the machinery accepted some authority which its makers neither fully controlled nor understood.

**Strong but limited evidence:** an answering will can use the Soterion to redirect loss, preserve privileges, and exploit the founders' exemptions. Its interventions are selective and constrained. It cannot make the failing world whole. Records of decay predate the local covenant, undermining claims that one malicious act invented entropy.

**Unresolved:** whether the Soterion contacted, hosted, or became part of the agency; where that agency began; whether its account of other dangers is truthful; what lies beyond the accessible channel; and whether human categories such as machine, person, or god describe it adequately.

The authorial world bible may know more than the characters. The encounter does not make that private cosmology common knowledge. History becomes clearer while ontology remains disturbing.

### Its voice and departure

The presence speaks sparingly. It can identify a precise sacrifice authorized earlier, revealing attention without claiming that it authored every decision. Its temptations concern actual shortages: a treatment restored for one person by assigning another household's losses, or a protected corridor made safe by granting continuing jurisdiction over its inhabitants.

Its offers must never be the only way to make the preceding campaign viable. Refusing cannot reveal that all prior victories were fake. Accepting a bargain, if supported, has explicit local terms; it is a distinct political choice, not an obscure dialogue trap.

When the interface fails, the Receiver briefly speaks without the inspection speakers. Its final proposed line is:

> “There are worse things coming. You will not get an answer from them.”

The connection breaks. No explanation follows. The statement may be threat, knowledge, spite, or manipulation. Do not immediately show the silhouette of a larger villain to certify its claim.

The battlefield then becomes quiet enough for ordinary work: casualty reports, shoring damaged passages, checking that the outside signal still arrives. The victory has a measurable result. This local channel can no longer override the separated institutions, and the defeated proxy does not reappear during the ending. Entropy and the rest of the world remain.

### Retreat, failure, and omission

Retreat preserves whatever evidence and personnel actually escape. Isolation can be partly successful even without expelling the presence. A failed assault can close access or worsen local service losses, but need not end the whole campaign if the outside coalition survives.

If the player does not enter, the ending may leave the deeper agency unconfirmed. Successful external severance can still deny its immediate reach. The optional fight earns a direct confrontation and stronger local security; it does not retroactively turn every other ending into an ignorant failure.

## 11. Endings and political consequences

The ending combines territorial and material facts with the settlement the player built. Avoid a single good/evil meter. Relevant dimensions include civilian authority, compulsory service, distribution of service access, treatment of defeated personnel, preservation of independent institutions, and the junction's remaining reach.

| Settlement | What was achieved | Remaining contradiction | Possible final scene |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A federation of passage** | Towns and service associations share enforceable rules while retaining rights of refusal | Slow coordination and unequal remaining resources require continuing politics | A civilian appeal overturns a military requisition, and the army complies |
| **The protected commonwealth** | A central public authority replaces private founder privileges | Emergency command remains powerful; its expiration must be made real | The general's seal is placed beside a dated instrument ending an emergency power |
| **The broken monopoly** | Communities remain independent and major coercive links are severed | Some services become less reliable; local exclusion can reproduce old injustice | Two former enemies negotiate use of a crossing neither can now command alone |
| **The continuity state** | The army preserves an integrated network and imposes regional order | People are safe on terms they cannot meaningfully refuse; liberation has become a new dependence | An official honors the caravan's memory while rejecting a travel claim under its founding emergency clause |
| **A surviving corridor** | The coalition fails to settle the whole basin but secures an enduring refuge and route | Powerful enemies and severe shortages remain outside and sometimes within it | A caravan arrives without surrendering the names of everyone it carries |

These are outcome families, with character and community variations. Killing an archon or expelling the proxy does not automatically select a political settlement. A materially diminished federation may be freer than a well-supplied continuity state; the narrative should describe both circumstances honestly.

Final images should give recognizable people a changed action: Halen choosing whether to travel, Petra's relatives deciding where to live, a workshop refusing requisition, a surrendered soldier returning home. Avoid concluding with an abstract world map whose colors stand in for all those lives.

## 12. Story delivery and simulation contracts

### Use the interfaces already established

- **Field atlas:** reports, observed movement, disputed borders, dated historical layers, and operational consequences. It remains the principal play surface.
- **Dispatch drawer:** proposals, surrender terms, time-sensitive requests, and arrival of testimony. Each request names its sender, destination, known stakes, and response deadline where one exists.
- **Orders workspace:** translates a political commitment into actual positions, targets, support, logistics, and contingencies. Promising evacuation creates transport and protection requirements on the same map.
- **Council scenes:** reserved for changes of authority, coalition commitments, and major disputes. Routine administration is delegated under established policy.
- **Personnel and relationship records:** show service history, known commitments, relevant relationships, and reasons for refusal. Avoid reducing political allegiance to a mystery percentage.
- **Archive:** separates primary evidence, testimony, interpretation, and unresolved claims. A revealed historical contradiction updates interpretation without erasing the original report.

This remains a command RPG, not a managerial simulation. The player approves priorities and exceptional commitments. Staff execute ordinary allocation, follow-up, and scheduling. A policy generates interruptions only for meaningful conflicts, changed conditions, or decisions outside delegated authority.

### Narrative state rules

Track discrete commitments and world facts: a surrender guarantee exists; a witness is alive and reachable; a workshop is independent; a court controls a relay; an allocation was exposed; a town has withdrawn consent. Relationships interpret those facts. Do not replace them with a universal loyalty score.

Each authored situation declares prerequisites, participants, triggering evidence, actual deadline, available orders, consequences, and fallback versions. Triggers use received information. A general cannot react to a death that has not been reported.

Per unit, existing command state machines continue to govern movement, combat, supply, and autonomous execution. Story logic changes objectives, available authority, and relationships; it does not bypass those systems to guarantee a scripted arrival. A promised rescue can fail because the force arrives too late. Its explanation must be visible in the world, not hidden in a scene counter.

### Death and missing characters

No named recruit is universally required after their introduction. Critical capabilities need organizational alternatives; crucial historical claims need more than one evidentiary path. The alternatives may be slower, more expensive, politically different, or less complete.

A death removes that person's relationships and future choices. A replacement engineer supplies engineering, not Tessa's personality and grief. If an important witness dies before giving testimony, the record remains incomplete unless other evidence exists. Political uncertainty is a valid consequence.

Report deaths, missing status, capture, and later corrections through the established observation system. Avoid a story window that reveals the death instantly while the military simulation still shows the unit out of contact.

### Sandbox pacing

Ordinary marches can take days. Regional fronts advance concurrently. The player chooses theaters, rests troops, secures transport, and decides which obligations to delay. Distances and force endurance constrain the plan without requiring constant consumption clicks.

Not every side story expires. Personal conversations can wait for plausible contact; a clinic evacuation under attack cannot. Distinguish those clearly. Repeatedly punishing the player for reading or speaking would undermine the cast-centered campaign.

Major transitions wait for an achieved state, such as a negotiated first settlement, not the completion of every optional operation. Before the generational handover, present the known commitments that will pass into the historical interval and let the player settle urgent matters where the world allows it.

## 13. Three anchor scenes

### Recognition after Three Crossings

The victory report includes a crossing abandoned to preserve the relief column. The saved towns disagree about which choice deserves commemoration.

**Asha:** “The companies call you General already. We are deciding what that permits.”

**Ione:** “They followed the orders before there was a document.”

**Asha:** “Then we are late.”

The player reviews the Passage Articles, appointments, and obligations. The scene makes command legitimate through achievement and consent without pretending that either has permanently settled the matter.

### Damar's proposed appointment

An operation would benefit from Damar's expertise. Tessa does not threaten to abandon every civilian unless the general agrees with her. She asks who can challenge his authority once he has it.

**Damar:** “I will submit to a hearing when the roads are secure.”

**Tessa:** “My brother was held until the roads were secure.”

**Ione:** “Then the date goes in the order.”

The player can make a bounded appointment, appoint someone else, or defer accountability with visible consequences. A good response does not close the relationship conflict; it establishes whether a promise has force.

### After the Receiver

The last warning ends. A runner asks whether the engineering party should recover equipment from the lower gallery or leave it to keep the withdrawal ahead of the failing supports.

This small order follows the cosmic confrontation deliberately. People still need a commander to make a limited, consequential decision with imperfect information. There is no speech announcing that hope has defeated entropy.

## 14. Writing and production boundaries

Keep the archons recognizable through conduct: ownership dressed as care, private exemption presented as universal freedom, spectacle during catastrophe, and promises of abundance whose costs are assigned elsewhere. Their familiar pettiness should sharpen rather than trivialize the harms. These fictional atrocities are not factual allegations about the contemporary figures informing the satire; the historical research remains documented in the World Bible's source ledger.

Give civilians and soldiers ordinary scenes before demanding grief for them. Preserve humor around songs, food, rival workshops, incompetent ceremonies, and the archons' need for approval. Never make displaced people the default punchline.

For production, prioritize the caravan through Three Crossings as the first complete narrative slice: six starting named figures, Reedbank's civilian ally, three potential early recruits, one surrender dispute, and recognition as general. It tests command, recruitment, politics, consequences, and the game's tone without requiring the full cosmology.

The complete campaign can then add the regional arcs, first settlement and handover, successor variations, and optional interior operation. Reuse locations after time and political change. An altered checkpoint on the same road is more expressive—and cheaper—than a succession of unrelated spectacular environments.

The governing promise is concrete: the world cannot recover everything it has lost, but the people living in it can defeat their owners, preserve relationships, and change who has the right to decide what happens next.
